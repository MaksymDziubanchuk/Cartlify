# Delivery: methods, zones, carriers, parcels

How an order gets to its buyer: by hand, picked up at the shop, or by a carrier of the delivery provider (ShippyPro). It is written for a developer who builds the frontend and for the shop owner; the bodies, the fields and every code are in Swagger (`/documentation`), the settings in [Shop settings](shop-settings.md), the provider and its keys in [Providers](providers.md) and [Configuration](configuration.md).

An order can be split into several parcels and each parcel into several boxes: a shop whose goods cannot travel in one box sends them anyway, and the app follows each parcel on its own.

## The three methods

| Method    | What it is                                                                                                          | Who sets the price                                                                   |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `manual`  | the shop sends the order itself, with any carrier, and writes the parcels by hand                                   | the zone of the country of the address (`manualPrice`)                               |
| `pickup`  | the buyer collects the order at the shop (the address and the hours of the settings)                                | free                                                                                 |
| `carrier` | a carrier of the delivery provider: the buyer picks one of the carriers the provider offers for the address and box | the provider (the rate tables the owner keeps in its dashboard), at the confirmation |

Root switches the methods on in `deliveryMethods`. `carrier` can be on only while the provider has its key (`SHIPPYPRO_API_KEY`) and the shop has the address the parcels leave from (`deliverySender`).

## Zones

The shop sells to the countries of its zones (`deliveryZones`), the same for every method that goes to an address. A zone is a name, its countries (or `rest`: every supported country no other zone names, one zone at most), the price of the delivery by hand to it (`null`: no delivery by hand there) and the sum of the cart from which the delivery is free (`freeFrom`, or `null`). A country is in one zone only.

- An address outside every zone is refused for every method that goes to an address (`DELIVERY_COUNTRY_NOT_ALLOWED`).
- From `freeFrom` the delivery by hand and the cheapest carrier cost the buyer nothing: the shop pays the carrier. Another carrier costs the buyer what it costs above the cheapest one (the shop pays the cheapest way, the buyer the wish for another).
- Example: Spain 5 € (free from 50 €), the European Union 12 €, the rest 30 €.

## Customs

A product may have its customs code (`hsCode`, 6 to 10 digits) and the country it was made in (`originCountry`). Whether a parcel to a country crosses customs is in the record of the country (`customsFree`, [Taxes](taxes.md)): the country of the shop and its customs union are free of customs. An address in a country with customs and a cart with a product that has no customs fields is refused, with the products named (`DELIVERY_CUSTOMS_DATA_MISSING`), both in the list of the prices and at the confirmation and again when the parcels are made; a pickup is not concerned. The provider gets the description, the units and the value of each line for the declaration, and makes the documents.

When the shop took the tax of the country of the recipient on the order, the parcel goes with the tax number of the shop there: a number of the import scheme (IOSS) with the parcel, any other number on a commercial invoice sent to the carrier with the parcel or printed by the admin (`customsInvoice` of the shipment, `GET /api/orders/:orderId/shipments/:shipmentId/customs-invoice`); a country with no number now refuses the parcel (`DELIVERY_TAX_NUMBER_MISSING`). See [Taxes](taxes.md), Parcels across customs.

## The buyer

1. The buyer fills the cart and gives the address. `POST /api/orders/current/delivery-options` answers every method the address can have, the cheapest first: the delivery by hand (when its zone has a price), the pickup, and each carrier with its price, its name for the buyer and its days. The server asks the provider again at every change of the address or of the cart; the prices are kept five minutes for an address and a box. A buyer asks the prices and confirms a limited number of times a minute (`LIMIT_DELIVERY_PRICES_PER_USER`, 30 unless set, `429 RATE_LIMITED`): the account of the provider takes a few requests a second for the whole shop.
2. When the carriers cannot be offered, `carriersUnavailable` says why, and the other methods stay: `not_enabled` (no carrier method), `shipping_data_missing` (a variant of the cart has no weight or sizes: no box to price), `provider_down` (the provider did not answer), `no_carrier_for_parcel` (the provider answered and prices no carrier for this parcel to this address: its weight is in no range of the rate tables, or the address in no zone of them; the log tells the owner). A shop whose only method is the carrier refuses the list with `503 DELIVERY_PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE`.
3. The buyer confirms with the method, the recipient, the address, the carrier picked (its line as the list gave it) and the price seen. The server asks the price again: a carrier gone is `DELIVERY_SERVICE_UNAVAILABLE`, another price is `DELIVERY_PRICE_CHANGED` with the new one (the page shows it and the buyer confirms again). A cart or a delivery that changed while the price was asked is not confirmed (`409 RESOURCE_BUSY`: the page confirms again and the checks see the cart as it is); a carrier is never confirmed without a price. The price is fixed from then on: the buyer never pays more, the difference with what the carrier really takes is the shop's in both ways.
4. The buyer pays the goods and the delivery on one page of Stripe; the page gets the address for the carrier too.
5. From the moment a carrier took a parcel, `delivery.shipments[]` of the order lists it (an order of several parcels shows the ones taken while it is still `paid` and waits for the others): its carrier, its tracking number and link, the numbers of its boxes when the carrier numbers each box (`boxNumbers`), where it stands (`sent`, `delivered`, `returning`, `returned`, `lost`). The mails of the shipment name every parcel.

A variant without weight or sizes cannot travel with a carrier. While other methods are on, the buyer still buys it with them. In a shop where the carrier is the only method, it is not sold: the catalog shows it without free units, adding it to the cart is `PRODUCT_NOT_DELIVERABLE`, and a cart that holds it is refused at the confirmation with the lines named (`ORDER_ITEMS_NOT_DELIVERABLE`). The admin sees these variants in the alert `products_without_shipping_data`.

## The admin: parcels and boxes

The desk of the orders lists the paid orders of a carrier that have no parcel yet (`GET /api/orders?parcels=none&statuses=paid`).

- **The draft.** `GET /api/orders/{orderId}/parcels/draft` gives the parcel the app would make: one box of every unit still to send, with the weight summed, the biggest length and width and the heights stacked, and the carrier the buyer picked.
- **Making the parcels.** `POST /api/orders/{orderId}/parcels` with `{}` makes the draft; with `parcels` the admin lays the units out over several parcels, each with its boxes (weight and sizes, the units of each item). Every unit that travels is in exactly one box (`PARCEL_ITEMS_MISMATCH` names the item that does not add up). The answer has the labels (PDF, with the address and the phone of the recipient), the tracking numbers (one a box when the carrier gives one) and the cost the provider counted, written as the expense of each parcel; the admin corrects it later when the bill of the carrier says another sum.
- **Several orders at once.** `POST /api/orders/parcels` makes the draft of each order named (20 at most in one request: each asks the provider for its labels in turn); one that fails does not stop the others, the answer tells each.
- **What refuses a parcel.** An order that is not paid, that something holds (`ORDER_ON_HOLD`), of another method, or whose units are all in live parcels (an order the carrier took a parcel of still gets parcels for the units left); an order to a country with customs whose product has no customs data (`DELIVERY_CUSTOMS_DATA_MISSING`, the products named: the countries without customs or the address may have changed since the buyer confirmed). Every parcel of the layout is checked before the first label is made. The order is read again once the provider has made a parcel: one that no longer suits it (a statement took units back or cancelled it, something holds it, its delivery was changed) is not kept, and is voided at the provider when its carrier can be asked (else the log names it for the admin to cancel in the dashboard of the carrier). A carrier that refuses (a street it does not know) answers `409 DELIVERY_PARCEL_REFUSED` with its words, nothing is written: the admin corrects the address (`PATCH /api/orders/{orderId}/delivery`) and makes it again. A carrier no longer connected: the admin picks another one of the current list (`GET /api/orders/{orderId}/carriers`: the carriers for the order now, with what each costs the shop) in the same `PATCH` (the price of the buyer stays).
- **When the provider does not answer.** `503`, nothing is written. A repeat first looks for a parcel the provider made under our number (`{orderId}-{n}`) and takes it when it has the boxes asked for now (as many, of the same weight), so an answer lost on the way never makes two parcels; one laid out another way carries another label: it is voided at the provider and the parcel is made anew.
- **Made wrong.** `POST /api/orders/{orderId}/parcels/void` voids the parcels not taken yet, or only one of them with `{ shipmentId }`, and the admin makes them again.
- **Sent without the provider.** The admin changes the method to `manual` (`PATCH .../delivery`, before any parcel is made) and sends it by hand; the price of the buyer stays.
- **Changing the delivery.** The label of a parcel carries the person, the address and the carrier: while the order has a parcel of the provider that is not voided, `PATCH .../delivery` changes only the note (`PARCELS_EXIST`); the admin voids the parcels first. An address in another country is checked for customs as the confirmation checks it.

A step by hand still works: `PATCH /api/orders/{orderId}/status` to `shipped` needs live parcels that hold every unit (`PARCELS_REQUIRED`): the ones only made count as taken from then, the ones the carrier took already keep their state and their time; `delivered` marks the ones on their way delivered. Late events change nothing. A step back to `paid` takes no parcel of a carrier back, since its label lives at the carrier: the parcels `sent` are only made again (`created`, no time of the send), their cost stays (`keepShippingCost` is not asked), and the events of the carrier move them from there; an order with a parcel the carrier moved further (`delivered`, `returning`, `returned`, `lost`) is not stepped back (`409 PARCEL_STATE_INVALID`). This is also the way out of an order the events could not move: its parcels were taken or delivered while something held it (the events move an order only when they come), or the event of one of them never came.

**A delivery by hand** has parcels and boxes the same way: the step to `shipped` takes `shipments[]`, each with its carrier, number, link, cost and boxes; the one parcel given without boxes gets one box of every unit. A step back takes every parcel back.

## The events of the carrier

The provider posts every event of a parcel to `POST /api/delivery/shippypro/webhook`, with the secret of the shop in the header `X-Webhook-Secret` (`401` without it, `403` with another one, `503` while the server has none to check with). A parcel moves forward only:

| Event of the provider  | The parcel  | The order                                                                                       |
| ---------------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| the carrier took it    | `sent`      | `shipped` once the carrier took every live parcel and they hold every unit; the buyer is mailed |
| delivered              | `delivered` | `delivered` once every live parcel is over, see below; the buyer is mailed                      |
| going back to the shop | `returning` | stays; the alert `parcels_coming_back`                                                          |
| back at the shop       | `returned`  | stays; the same alert                                                                           |
| lost                   | `lost`      | stays; the alert `parcels_lost`                                                                 |

The order is `delivered` once every live parcel is over (delivered, back at the shop, lost), one of them at least is delivered, and a statement holds the units of the ones that did not reach the buyer: at the time of the last delivery. Until then a parcel back at the shop or lost keeps the order `shipped` (its alert asks the admin what became of the units), and the statement of the admin moves the order when it was the last thing missing. A parcel going back that the carrier delivers after all is `delivered`; a parcel back at the shop or lost does not change any more.

A delivery that came without the pickup takes both steps. A repeat, an event back, an event of a voided parcel or of a parcel the shop does not know, and a body that is no event the app knows (a kind the provider added, a test of the webhook) change nothing and are answered `200`: any other answer and the provider posts it again. The moment of an event is never of the future and never before the order was paid (a date of the provider that is none), since the cost of the send is counted from it. An order something holds is not moved, its parcel is (the alert `parcels_ahead_of_order`). The order of the events of one order is kept: they go one after another.

## Voids

A parcel the carrier never took is voided when its order is cancelled (any way: a statement before the sending, a decision on a hold, a dispute) and when the admin voids it to make it again. It waits for its void (`void_pending`), and a worker asks the provider every minute to void the ones of a carrier that can (every waiting parcel in turn: the ones left for the admin never keep the others from theirs); then it is `voided`, the shop owes the carrier nothing: the carrier gives its cost back, a line `money.shipping_refund` of the money journal in the period of the void (the cost stays in the period it was written in). A carrier that cannot be asked (or a provider that does not answer) leaves it waiting: the alert `parcels_void_pending` tells the admin to cancel it in the dashboard of the carrier and confirm (`POST /api/orders/{orderId}/shipments/{shipmentId}/void-confirmation`). A parcel the carrier takes while it waits is sent all the same (the alert `parcels_ahead_of_order` when its order is cancelled).

## Statements and parcels

- **A statement before the carrier took any parcel.** The units it names come back to the stock at once, as for any order not sent; the parcels not taken that hold them wait for their void, the others stay, and the admin makes a parcel of what is left (the draft shows it).
- **A statement once the carrier took a parcel.** The order counts as a sent one: the units still at the shop (in parcels not taken, or in none) come back at once and their parcels wait for their void; the units on their way are expected back as from any sent order. The order is cancelled once every unit and its money are back.
- **Refused at the door.** A provider that tells the refusal opens the statement of the refusal itself (`source = system`, `reason = refused`, the moment of the event): the units of the parcels coming back that no statement holds. ShippyPro does not tell why a parcel goes back: the alert `parcels_coming_back` asks the admin, who tells the refusal (`POST /api/returns` with `reason = refused` and the moment the carrier gives) or waits for the parcel to be back. The admin may tell it before any event too (the provider may skip the state, or never send it), once the buyer or the carrier says so: the parcel is still `sent`. A refusal voids no parcel that is only made: its units are the ones of the parcels the carrier took. The refusal of the admin takes every parcel not delivered (`sent`, `returning`, `returned`), not the ones delivered; the one a provider tells takes only the parcels coming back. While a refusal is open the order does not become `delivered`: a parcel the carrier still delivers is `delivered`, its order stays `shipped`, and the statement waits for the goods as any. A buyer who withdrew while the parcel was on its way keeps the statement: the parcel only goes back.
- **Not collected.** While the parcel waits at the carrier, the goods are the buyer's. **A parcel the carrier brings back because the buyer did not collect it is a refusal**: the admin confirms the return and tells the refusal with the date the parcel came back; the money goes back as for a refusal at the door, less the way back if the terms of the shop say so. Put this into the terms of the shop.
- **Lost.** The alert `parcels_lost` names the order; the admin opens a statement with the reason `other` for the units of its boxes (no deduction), the buyer gets the money, and the order is `delivered` by its other parcels once the statement holds the lost units; claiming the parcel from the carrier is the shop's business. A carrier that says "delivered" while the buyer says nothing came: the admin handles it by hand, with the proof of the delivery from the carrier.

## Alerts

| Kind                     | A case                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `parcels_not_collected`  | an order with a parcel made some days ago (3 unless set) and not taken by the carrier                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `parcels_void_pending`   | an order with a parcel waiting for its void                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `parcels_coming_back`    | an order with a parcel going back to the shop or back at it, with units no statement holds                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `parcels_lost`           | an order with a parcel the carrier lost, with units no statement holds                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `parcels_ahead_of_order` | a cancelled order a parcel of went after the cancel, a paid order something holds the carrier took a parcel of, a paid order the carrier has every live parcel of and that is not shipped, a shipped order with an open refusal whose live parcels were all delivered |
| `delivery_provider_down` | the provider has not answered for some seconds (900 unless set): every call to it tells it, the first failure after its last answer is the time (`occurredAt`); an hour with no call after the last failure ends it (nobody knows it is still down)                   |
| `shipped_orders_waiting` | an order sent by hand or by a carrier and not delivered for some days                                                                                                                                                                                                 |

What the admin does for `parcels_ahead_of_order`: a cancelled order whose parcel went anyway is a refusal or a return to arrange with the buyer; a paid order whose parcels the carrier took is sent by hand (the step above) once nothing holds it; a paid order with a unit in no parcel gets its units back to the buyer by a statement, or the admin voids what can be voided and makes the parcels again; a shipped order delivered under an open refusal is checked with the buyer (the refusal may be none): once its statement is closed, the admin steps the order to `delivered` by hand.

Each kind with cases names the list of orders with the filter `parcels` (`not_collected`, `void_pending`, `coming_back`, `lost`, `ahead`), so the count of the alert is the total of its list.

A statement names items and units, not parcels, so `parcels_coming_back` and `parcels_lost` compare the units of an item in the parcel with the units of that item in all the statements of the order. When one item is laid out in several parcels and a statement already holds some of its units, a parcel lost or coming back with that item may give no alert: the card of the order shows the state of each parcel.

## Personal data

The provider and its carriers get only what a parcel needs: the name, the address, the phone and the email of the recipient (the carriers send the notices of the delivery), and the lines of the order with their value for the declaration. They are recipients of the personal data of the buyer: the privacy policy of the shop names them, and the owner signs the data processing agreement of the provider. The label of a parcel carries the name, the address and the phone: its link, and the links and the numbers of its boxes, are deleted when the recipient of the order is (the cleanup of the personal data of the orders).

## For the owner: the dashboard of ShippyPro

- **The carriers.** Connect the contracts of your carriers in the dashboard of ShippyPro; the app knows only ShippyPro and offers every carrier it returns for the address and the box. The currency of the account must be the one of the shop: a price in another currency is left out.
- **The rate tables.** Write in them what each carrier charges you: the price the buyer pays and the expense in the reports come from them. Where a carrier is offered is decided only by the zones of the table (ShippyPro does not check the route), and the weight steps must leave no gap.
- **One account an installation.** The numbers the app gives its parcels (`{orderId}-{n}`) are unique in one database only, and the account has one webhook: a test or staging copy of the shop needs an account of its own, never the one of production.
- **The webhook.** In API → Webhook, the address `https://<the domain of the API>/api/delivery/shippypro/webhook`, a custom header `X-Webhook-Secret` with the value of `SHIPPYPRO_WEBHOOK_SECRET` (a random text of at least 32 characters: the server does not start with a shorter one), and the events Order Shipped, Order Error and Tracking Update.
- **The pickup.** The app does not call a courier: set a regular pickup in the dashboard of ShippyPro or of the carrier, or bring the parcels to the office.
- **The first real parcel.** Compare what came to the webhook (taken, delivered) with the parcel; check the number of each box and the lines of the customs on a real carrier.

## Not done

Cash on delivery, pickup points and lockers, the tracking on the way, turning a parcel back on its way, calling a courier, return labels, checking the address with the provider, the weight in the price of the delivery by hand.
