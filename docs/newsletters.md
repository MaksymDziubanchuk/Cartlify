# Newsletters

This page explains the marketing newsletters of the shop to a developer who builds the frontend and to the shop owner: who can subscribe and how, the pages behind the links of the mails, how a campaign is written, scheduled and sent, what the people who run the newsletters see, what happens to the data, and what the owner sets up outside the code. Every statement here is true of the code; the routes, the fields and the error codes are in [API](api.md) and in Swagger (`/documentation`), the environment variables are in [configuration](configuration.md), the data of the shop is in [shop settings](shop-settings.md) and the languages are in [languages](languages.md).

## The model in one page

- **A subscription is a row of its own**, in tables apart from the accounts: a subscription (the address, the source, the language, the state), the journal of its events (only added to), the campaigns, the mails of a campaign (a row for each subscriber it went to) and the blocklist of the public form. Nothing about a subscription is a column of `users`, except the right of an admin to run the newsletters.
- **Two kinds of subscription** (`source`). `account`: a signed-in user subscribed the address of their own account; it is in force at once, no mail, no captcha. `form`: anybody typed an address into the public form; it is in force only after the owner of the mailbox confirms it by the link of a mail (double opt-in).
- **One subscription in force per address.** While one is waiting for its confirmation, in force or paused, a second cannot be made; the one in force is changed or ended, and a new one is made after that.
- **One list and one unsubscribe.** There are no topics. A mail of a campaign is the only mail of the newsletters apart from the two mails of the subscription itself.
- **The language** is kept in the subscription and does not follow the language of the account. `null` means "no language": the mails go in the language of the shop at the moment each mail is built.

| State of a subscription (`status`) | Meaning                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `pending`                          | a request of the form waits for the confirmation (the link lives 3 days, then the request is removed)              |
| `active`                           | in force: the campaigns that start now are mailed to it                                                            |
| `paused_complaint`                 | the person marked a mail as spam: no mail is sent until the person resumes it                                      |
| `stopped_bounce`                   | the mail provider says the address does not exist: no mail is sent, the person may subscribe again by the form     |
| `ended`                            | over; the row and its journal stay as the proof of the consent for the term the privacy policy gives, then they go |

The reason a subscription ended (`endReason`): `unsubscribed`, `account_deleted`, `removed_by_admin`, `expired`, `not_me`, `bounced`.

| State of a campaign | Meaning                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `draft`             | being written; may be incomplete                                                                                                                                   |
| `scheduled`         | has a time; the sending pass starts it when the time comes                                                                                                         |
| `sending`           | the mails are going out                                                                                                                                            |
| `finished`          | every mail was answered for                                                                                                                                        |
| `cancelled`         | a scheduled campaign that was called off: by a manager, or by the system when the limit of the shop left no room for it as its time came (`cancelReason: 'limit'`) |
| `stopped`           | one that was going out and was stopped: whoever has not been sent the mail yet will not be                                                                         |

## Rules for the frontend

- **The token of a page behind a link is in the fragment, after the `#`**, never in the query: `<page>#token=<token>` (the page that confirms an email: `#token=<token>&newsletter=1` when the box was ticked at the registration; the page of the unsubscribe button: `#unsubscribe=<token>&c=<campaign id>`, below). A fragment reaches no server, so no log of the host, of a proxy or of a mail scanner holds it. The page reads it with `new URLSearchParams(location.hash.slice(1))`.
- **Opening a page behind a link does nothing.** A mail scanner opens every link of a mail, so a page reads the token from `location.hash` and shows a button; only the button sends the request (confirm, "this is not me", unsubscribe, the choice "subscribe" on the page that confirms an email, which is a `POST /api/auth/verify`). The token goes in the body of the request (for the unsubscribe button, in the path of its own address, below).
- **The answer of the public form is always the same.** Whatever the state of the address is, the form gets `202` with one fixed message; the page always says "check your mailbox". Never try to read the state of an address from the answer. The mail goes out after the answer, so the answer also takes as long whatever is mailed.
- **Keep the token out of the address bar and out of other sites.** The token of "Manage the subscription" lives as long as the subscription. The pages behind the links read the fragment and at once take it out of the address (`history.replaceState`), and they are served with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and load nothing of an analytics service, so the token reaches no history, no `Referer` and no log of another site.
- **A page opened with `#unsubscribe=…` shows ONLY the button "Unsubscribe".** Not the state, not the address, not the other buttons of the page "Manage the subscription": the token in that fragment is the one of the unsubscribe button of a mail program and opens nothing else. The button sends a `POST` to the same address the mail program would have posted to, `/api/newsletters/unsubscribe/<token>?c=<id>` (below). The page "Manage the subscription" with `#token=…` is the whole page; with `#unsubscribe=…` it is this one button.
- **Warn before a translation.** `POST /api/newsletters/translate` sends the texts to OpenAI. The page says so before the first request: "the texts go to OpenAI, do not put personal data in them". The privacy policy does not change for it (the text of a newsletter is not personal data).
- **Warn about the blocks that choose products by a rule.** A block of the rule `bestsellers` or `top_rated` may take products that have no sales or no ratings yet (the rule is the catalog sorted by sales or by rating, and its first cards up to the limit are taken, so when fewer products have any, the rest are products without). The page tells the person who writes the campaign to check the test mail before scheduling.
- **Every `POST`, `PATCH` and `DELETE` carries the CSRF token** (`GET /api/auth/csrf`, the header `x-csrf-token`, with the cookie), except the one-click unsubscribe of a mail program, whose address is outside the check (the page of the unsubscribe button needs no token for it). The pages behind the links need no session. A `429` carries `Retry-After`.
- **The widget of the captcha gives a new answer for every attempt**, also after a refusal: an answer works once.
- **Refresh `canManageNewsletters` at every opening of the app** with `GET /api/users/me` (see "The people who run the newsletters"). Never trust a value kept in `localStorage` or a cookie.
- **Show `html` of a preview only in an `iframe` with `sandbox`**, never in the DOM of the admin pages.
- **Languages come from the shop.** The list of languages a person may pick is `enabledLocales` of `GET /api/shop-settings`.

## The pages the frontend builds

The server links to these pages from the mails. The paths are settings (`WEB_PAGE_*`, a path of the site from `/`, the defaults below; a value may not hold a `#`), and every page reads its token from the fragment of its address (`location.hash`). **The default paths are the contract with the frontend**: the server builds the links with them whenever a setting is not set, so the frontend serves a page at each default path, or the owner sets the `WEB_PAGE_*` of the environment to the paths the frontend has.

| Setting                       | Default path          | Opened from                                                                                        | What the page does                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `WEB_PAGE_NEWSLETTER_CONFIRM` | `/newsletter/confirm` | the button "Confirm the subscription" of the mail of a confirmation                                | a button that sends `POST /api/newsletters/subscriptions/confirm` with `{ token }`; `200 { status: 'active', locale }`                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| `WEB_PAGE_NEWSLETTER_DECLINE` | `/newsletter/decline` | the button "This is not me, do not send more" of the same mail                                     | a button that sends `POST /api/newsletters/subscriptions/decline` with `{ token }` (the same token); `200 { blocked: true }`                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| `WEB_PAGE_NEWSLETTER_MANAGE`  | `/newsletter/manage`  | "Manage the subscription" in every mail of a campaign and in the mail "You are already subscribed" | with `#token=…`: opens with `POST /api/newsletters/manage` `{ token }` and shows the address, the language and the state; buttons for the language, the unsubscribe, the resume and the export (below). With `#unsubscribe=…&c=…` (the unsubscribe button of a mail program leads here): ONLY the button "Unsubscribe" (below) |
| `WEB_PAGE_VERIFY_EMAIL`       | `/web/login`          | the mail "Confirm your email address"                                                              | the page that confirms an email reads `#token=…` (and the hint `newsletter=1`), shows the choice "Subscribe to the newsletter" and a button that sends `POST /api/auth/verify` (below)                                                                                                                                         |
| `WEB_PAGE_PRODUCT`            | `/products/{id}`      | a card of a product in a mail of a campaign                                                        | the page of a product (`{id}` once)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| `WEB_PAGE_CATEGORY`           | `/categories/{id}`    | a block of a category in a mail of a campaign                                                      | the page of a category (`{id}` once)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

A token that nobody was given, a subscription that is over, or a link of a test mail is the same answer for a page: `400 NEWSLETTER_LINK_INVALID`. The page says "the subscription was not found" for all of them; it cannot tell them apart, and neither can an attacker. A link of a confirmation that was used also answers so: if the person confirmed already, they are subscribed. The one exception is the unsubscribe: for a token that was given and whose subscription is over already it answers `200` again, so a double press harms nothing.

**The page "Manage the subscription"** sends the token in the body of every request (the address of the request never holds it):

| Request                                    | Body                | Answer                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `POST /api/newsletters/manage`             | `{ token }`         | `{ email, source, locale, status, canChangeLocale }`; `status` is `active` or `paused_complaint`                                          |
| `POST /api/newsletters/manage/locale`      | `{ token, locale }` | the same state; only a subscription of the form (`canChangeLocale: true`), else `409 NEWSLETTER_LOCALE_NOT_ALLOWED`; `400 LOCALE_INVALID` |
| `POST /api/newsletters/manage/unsubscribe` | `{ token }`         | `{ unsubscribed: true }`, also when it is over already                                                                                    |
| `POST /api/newsletters/manage/resume`      | `{ token }`         | the state; for a subscription paused by a complaint. The server also takes the address off the list of complaints of the mail provider    |
| `POST /api/newsletters/manage/export`      | `{ token }`         | `{ subscriptions: [...] }` with the subscription and its journal; the page offers it as a download                                        |

Show the button for the language only when `canChangeLocale` is true (a subscription of an account changes its language in the profile), and the button "resume" only when `status` is `paused_complaint`. The list of languages is `enabledLocales`.

## The public subscription form

`POST /api/newsletters/subscriptions`, for a guest and for a signed-in user. The body is `{ email?, locale?, captchaToken? }` (it may be left out altogether for a signed-in user).

**Anybody about an address nobody has proved to be theirs** (a guest, or a signed-in user about another address) sends `email`, `captchaToken` and `locale`, **the language is required of both**: it is a language the shop answers in, and whoever sends none or another gets `400 LOCALE_INVALID`, no other is put in its place (the mail goes to the owner of the address, and the language of the account of the person who asks is no language of theirs). The order of the checks: the schema, the language when one is sent (a wrong one is `400 LOCALE_INVALID`), the email (`400 EMAIL_REQUIRED` for a guest without one), a language there at all (none is `400 LOCALE_INVALID`), then the captcha (`400 CAPTCHA_INVALID`, `503 CAPTCHA_UNAVAILABLE`), then the limit of the email (below), then the request is recorded. The answer is `202 { message }`, a fixed sentence, whatever happened. By the state of the address a mail may follow (always after the request is saved, and a mail the provider does not take is only logged, so it never changes the answer):

| State of the address                          | What is mailed                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| nothing, an ended or a bounced subscription   | "Confirm the subscription" (two buttons: confirm, "This is not me"), in the language of the request                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| a request waiting for its confirmation        | the same mail again with a new link (the old one stops working, the 3 days start again, the language is the new request's)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| a subscription of the form in force or paused | "You are already subscribed" with the link "Manage the subscription", in the language of that subscription. For a **paused** one (`paused_complaint`) another text: the subscription was paused after a mark "spam", and the person resumes it on the page "Manage the subscription"; before it the server takes the address off the list of complaints of the SendGrid account of the newsletters (the provider drops every mail to an address on it), and a failure of that call is only logged and the mail still goes |
| a subscription of an account in force         | nothing (its owner sees it in the profile)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| the address is on the blocklist of the form   | nothing                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

Whatever the table says, an address gets **at most one mail of the two kinds a day**: a second request within a day is recorded as nothing and mails nothing. **The mark of the day is lifted when the mail never left**: if the provider does not take the mail (or the server cannot build it), the server calls the database to take the mark back, so the person can ask again at once instead of losing the day. It does that only while the mark is under 5 minutes old, only for the kind that failed, and a failure of that call is only logged.

**The limits.** A request from one client address is limited (10 in 15 minutes) before the captcha. The limit of the same email (1 in 3 minutes) is counted **after** the captcha, **by mailbox** (an address in any case, with a `+tag`, with or without the dots of a Gmail name, `googlemail.com` for `gmail.com`, counts as the one it writes: the way the blocklist folds an address), and only for an address that is not the person's own: a request that fails the captcha, or a signed-in user subscribing their own address, spends none of it, so a script without a captcha cannot keep a person from the one mail they may ask for. Over a limit: `429 RATE_LIMITED` with `Retry-After`. The same two limits stand on `POST /api/auth/verify/resend` and `POST /api/auth/password/forgot`, in the same order (the address of the client, the captcha, the email), the email by mailbox as well (the limits of the login count an address as it is written: a `+tag` is another account there); `POST /api/auth/register` has the limit of the address only (`LIMIT_REGISTER_PER_ADDRESS`, 10 in an hour, before the captcha). The mail of a confirmation says the link lives 3 days; after that the request is removed.

**A signed-in user for their own address** (no `email`, or the address of the account) needs no captcha and gets no mail: `201` with the subscription (the object `GET /api/newsletters/me` gives), in force at once. `locale` is optional (the language asked, else the one of the account if the shop answers in it, else none). `409 NEWSLETTER_ALREADY_SUBSCRIBED` when a subscription is in force on the address already, whoever made it; `403 NEWSLETTER_EMAIL_NOT_VERIFIED` when the account has not verified its email (a subscription sits on an address, and only its proved owner sees or changes it). A request of the form for that address that waited for its confirmation becomes the subscription of the account. The blocklist does not touch this path.

**"This is not me"** puts the address on the blocklist of the form (kept as a keyed hash, with the mark "the person") and removes the request. After it a request of the form for that address gets the same `202` and no mail, while a signed-in owner of the address can still subscribe from the profile. An admin may block an address too (and lift the block on the word of the person, who writes to support).

**The blocklist knows a mailbox, not the text that was typed.** The keyed hash (HMAC with `NEWSLETTER_BLOCK_SECRET`) is made from the address cut down to the mailbox: everything from the first `+` is dropped when something stands before it (`a+1@x.com` is `a@x.com`, on every domain), and for Gmail (`gmail.com` and `googlemail.com`) the dots of the name are dropped too and `googlemail.com` is read as `gmail.com` (`a.n.n.a@googlemail.com` is `anna@gmail.com`). So an address that was blocked stays blocked when typed another way. The form, "this is not me" and the admin who blocks or looks an address up all go through the same rule. The subscription itself keeps the address as it was typed.

## The profile

For a signed-in user of any role (guest `403 INSUFFICIENT_ROLE`; an account with an unconfirmed email `403 NEWSLETTER_EMAIL_NOT_VERIFIED` on all four):

| Route                             | What                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `GET /api/newsletters/me`         | `{ subscribed: false }` or `{ subscribed: true, source, status, locale, canChangeLocale, subscribedAt }`: the subscription in force on the address of the account, also one made by the form                 |
| `PATCH /api/newsletters/me`       | `{ locale }`: only a subscription of an account (`409 NEWSLETTER_LOCALE_NOT_ALLOWED` for one of the form, which changes its language on the page "Manage the subscription"); `404 NEWSLETTER_NOT_SUBSCRIBED` |
| `DELETE /api/newsletters/me`      | ends the subscription; `{ unsubscribed: true }`, the same when there is none                                                                                                                                 |
| `POST /api/newsletters/me/resume` | resumes a subscription paused by a complaint (`404 NEWSLETTER_NOT_SUBSCRIBED` when there is none in force)                                                                                                   |

The box "Subscribe to the newsletter" of the profile sends `POST /api/newsletters/subscriptions` without an `email`; an unticked box sends `DELETE /api/newsletters/me`. After the subscription is ended the box makes a subscription of the account again.

## At the registration and at the login

- **Registration with a password.** The form sends the box as an optional boolean `newsletter` in the body of `POST /api/auth/register`. It is stored nowhere and subscribes nobody; it only adds `newsletter=1` to the link of the mail "Confirm your email address" (the mail sent again by `POST /api/auth/verify/resend` carries no hint). The link of the mail is `WEB_PAGE_VERIFY_EMAIL#token=<token>` (with `&newsletter=1` when the box was ticked). The page reads `token` and `newsletter` from `location.hash`, shows the box "Subscribe to the newsletter" (ticked when the hint is there) and a button; the button sends **`POST /api/auth/verify`** with the body `{ token, newsletter: true | false }` and the CSRF token (there is no `GET /api/auth/verify`: opening a link must verify nobody). When that call is the one that confirms the email and the choice is true, the account is subscribed (place `registration`); a choice that is not a boolean is `400 VALIDATION_ERROR` before anything changes, `400 VERIFY_TOKEN_INVALID_OR_EXPIRED` is a link that is wrong, used, expired or not the newest one. **The page must send the choice only from the button, never when it loads.**
- **The answer says nothing about the subscription.** `{ message: 'Email verified' }` is the same whether the address was subscribed already (the one in force stays, no second one is made) or not, so the answer cannot be used to find out who is subscribed. A subscription that cannot be made never undoes the confirmation (it is logged without the address).
- **Login with Google, GitHub or LinkedIn.** The page adds `newsletter=true` to the start (`GET /api/auth/oauth/google|github|linkedin`), next to `acceptedDocumentIds`. It travels in the signed `state` and subscribes the account only when that login **creates a new account**; a login to an account the shop has ignores it. The language of the subscription is the language of the account if the shop answers in it.
- **The captcha** stands on `POST /api/auth/register`, `POST /api/auth/password/forgot`, `POST /api/auth/verify/resend` and the form above (not on the login and not on the providers). The widget of Cloudflare Turnstile gives an answer that the page sends in the body field `captchaToken` (required, up to 2048 characters, good once). `400 CAPTCHA_INVALID`: the answer is missing, wrong, expired or used before; `503 CAPTCHA_UNAVAILABLE`: Cloudflare did not answer in time or failed, or the secret of the shop is not taken. The request is then refused and nothing was done, never let through; reset the widget, take a new answer and repeat.

## The unsubscribe button of a mail program

Every mail of a campaign carries two headers: `List-Unsubscribe: <https://<api>/api/newsletters/unsubscribe/<unsubscribe token>?c=<campaign id>>` and `List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click` (RFC 8058). When the reader presses the button of their mail program (Gmail, Apple Mail), the program sends a `POST` to that address by itself, with no cookie and no CSRF token, and expects the subscription to end at once.

- **The token of this button is its own.** It is not the token of the page "Manage the subscription" (below): it is another HMAC of the same salt (with the mark `:unsubscribe`), the database keeps only its hash in a column of its own, and a database function that can end the subscription and do nothing else looks it up. The header sits in every mail and shows in the logs of the proxies and of the mail clients, so it must open the unsubscribe and nothing more: with the page token one could open the export of the subscription (the address and the browser of its journal). The two tokens do not open each other's routes (`400 NEWSLETTER_LINK_INVALID`).
- `POST /api/newsletters/unsubscribe/:token?c=<campaign id>`: no session, no captcha, the body of the form of the standard may be left out. It is outside the CSRF check (`csrf: false`): a mail program cannot take a token first. `200 { unsubscribed: true }`, also for a subscription that is over (a mail program may post twice); `400 NEWSLETTER_LINK_INVALID` only for a token nobody was given. `c` is only used to count the unsubscribe against the campaign; anything that is not the id of a campaign, and the id of a campaign that never mailed this subscription, is ignored (the unsubscribe works, nothing is counted). The journal keeps the place `mail_client`.
- `GET /api/newsletters/unsubscribe/:token?c=<campaign id>` is for a person (or a scanner) who opens the address of the header: it answers **`303` to `WEB_PAGE_NEWSLETTER_MANAGE#unsubscribe=<token>&c=<id>`** (`c` only when the address names an id that can be read), with `Cache-Control: no-store`. It changes nothing and looks nothing up, so it tells nothing about the token (a wrong token is learned from the `POST`), and a scanner that opens it unsubscribes nobody. The page it leads to shows the one button "Unsubscribe" (see "Rules for the frontend") and posts to the same address.
- The token is in the path, so every log hides it. The `POST` and the `GET` share one count of their own, `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_UNSUBSCRIBE_PER_ADDRESS` (1000 in 10 minutes from one address, wide because a mailbox provider unsubscribes many readers from the few addresses of its servers), apart from the pages behind the links (`LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_LINK_PER_ADDRESS`, 60 in 10 minutes). The general limit of the API (`RATE_LIMIT_MAX`) does not count these two routes: only the count of the button holds, so a mailbox provider that unsubscribes a crowd of readers from one address is refused only at `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_UNSUBSCRIBE_PER_ADDRESS` (`429 RATE_LIMITED` with `details.limit: newsletterUnsubscribe`).
- The headers are in every mail of a campaign and **only** in them; the two mails of the subscription itself carry none.

## The token of the link "Manage the subscription"

The link is `WEB_PAGE_NEWSLETTER_MANAGE#token=<token>`: the token is in the fragment, so no server sees it. The database keeps only a hash of the token. The server derives the token of a subscription again for every mail with an HMAC of a random salt of the row and `NEWSLETTER_LINK_SECRET`, so the link is the same in every mail, works as long as the subscription is in force or paused (`active`, `paused_complaint`), and a copy of the database without the secret opens no link. The token of the unsubscribe button (above) is made from the same salt and the same secret with another mark, so it is another value. **`NEWSLETTER_LINK_SECRET` is never changed after the launch**: every link in the mails already sent, of both kinds, would stop working.

The token of a confirmation is another one: random, kept as a hash, valid 3 days, replaced by a new one with every new mail of a confirmation, and the same for both buttons of the mail (confirm and "This is not me").

## Complaints and bounces

The mails of the newsletters go through a SendGrid stream of their own (below), and its Event Webhook posts the events to `POST /api/newsletters/sendgrid/webhook`.

| Event                                  | What the server does                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `spamreport` (a complaint)             | the mail is marked as a complaint, the subscription is paused (`paused_complaint`) and no mail is sent to it; the campaign counts a complaint      |
| `bounce` (the address does not exist)  | the subscription is stopped (`stopped_bounce`, reason `bounced`); the campaign counts an undelivered mail                                          |
| `bounce` with `type: blocked`          | counted as a drop (the mail did not arrive) and the subscription stays: the receiving server refused it for a reason that is not about the address |
| `dropped`                              | the campaign counts an undelivered mail; the subscription stays, unless SendGrid says why (below)                                                  |
| anything else (`delivered`, `open`...) | acknowledged and left alone                                                                                                                        |

A `dropped` event carries a `reason`, the words SendGrid gives for dropping the mail (read for `dropped` only, trimmed and cut to 200 characters, compared without regard to case). Two reasons mean that the address is one SendGrid already knows as bad, so the subscription is dealt with as the event of that reason would deal with it, and the campaign still counts an undelivered mail (never a complaint). The reason `Bounced Address` is checked against a live event of SendGrid:

| `reason` of a `dropped` event | The subscription                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `Bounced Address`             | as for a `bounce`: stopped (`stopped_bounce`, reason `bounced`) if in force or paused |
| `Spam Reporting Address`      | as for a `spamreport`: a subscription in force is paused (`paused_complaint`)         |
| any other, or none            | stays (`Unsubscribed Address` and the like are only counted)                          |

- **A complaint is not an unsubscribe.** The person sees the pause on the page "Manage the subscription" or in the profile (`status: 'paused_complaint'`) and resumes with one press. The server then takes the address off the list of complaints of the SendGrid account of the newsletters (`DELETE /v3/suppression/spam_reports/{email}`, with the key of the newsletters; if that call fails it is logged and the pause is still lifted), so the provider stops dropping the mails. The server never resumes a subscription itself: mail providers take a sender who mails people who complained for a spammer. Gmail does not report single complaints (only a share in Postmaster Tools), so the complaints that arrive come from Outlook, Yahoo and the like.
- **The webhook** is no route of the frontend. It is signed with ECDSA over P-256 (the headers `X-Twilio-Email-Event-Webhook-Signature` and `X-Twilio-Email-Event-Webhook-Timestamp`) and checked against `SENDGRID_NEWSLETTER_WEBHOOK_KEY` before the body is read; it has no CSRF, no session and no request limit, like the webhook of Stripe. A request more than 25 hours old or 5 minutes ahead, a wrong signature or a missing header is refused (`400`/`401`), a body over 4 MB `413`, anything but JSON `415`; a Verification Key that is blank or not P-256 is `503 SENDGRID_WEBHOOK_KEY_UNAVAILABLE` (on production the server does not start with such a key). The answer is `{ received, applied, skipped }`. When the database fails on an event the others are still stored and the answer is a `5xx`, so SendGrid delivers the batch again for up to a day; applying an event twice changes nothing.
- **An Event Webhook hears every event of its SendGrid account**: the mails of the orders, if they go through the same account, and the mails of another environment that uses the account (a development account may serve several). Every mail of a campaign carries three custom arguments (the campaign, the recipient and the environment: `prod`, `dev` or `test`); an event counts only when all three are there and the environment is this one. A mail sent before the environment was marked is not recognised and its events are left alone.

## Deleting an account

`DELETE /api/users/me` (and the deletion by root) ends what is in force on the address of the account in the same transaction, before the address is made anonymous: the subscription of the account, and the subscription of the form on the same address (the latter only when the account had proved the address; a request that still waits is left to its three days). The mail "Your account was deleted" says "Your newsletter subscription has been cancelled as well" in the language of the account, only when something ended. **The page asks for the confirmation of the deletion and must say that the subscription ends too.** The ended subscription and its journal stay as the proof of the consent for the term of the privacy policy. The subscription of the form that the deletion ended is marked with the id of the account (a column that no answer shows), because after the deletion the account has no address of its own to find its subscriptions by: the export of a deleted account finds the subscriptions it made by `userId` and these by that mark.

## The data of a person

- **The export** of an account (`GET /api/users/me/export`; for another person or a deleted account `GET /api/users/:userId/export`, root) has the section `newsletterSubscriptions`: **only the subscriptions the person confirmed** (a subscription of the account, and a subscription of the form that has the time of its confirmation) with their journals (the event, the place, the time, the IP address and the browser, the version of the privacy policy it was agreed under, and the campaign an unsubscribe came from). A request of the form that waits for its confirmation, or that ended without ever being confirmed, is not given: it is the doing of whoever typed the address, and its IP address and browser are theirs, not the person's. Never a token, a hash or a salt. An empty list for an account with none, for one that never proved its address and for one whose subscriptions were never confirmed. For a deleted account (root) the subscriptions are found by the id of the account (above). Without an account the page "Manage the subscription" offers the same as a download (`POST /api/newsletters/manage/export`): the same rule, the subscription behind the link and its journal.
- **Every event of consent** (the request, the confirmation, the subscription) keeps the version of the privacy policy in force at that moment.

## The people who run the newsletters

**The right.** Root runs the newsletters. An admin does only when root gave the right (`PATCH /api/root/admins/:adminId` with `{ "canManageNewsletters": true | false }`, a strict JSON boolean; the answer is the admin as the list `GET /api/root/admins` shows it). It works at once and stops at once, also in a session that is open. The right is a column that no statement of the app can write (only root's function of the database does), it goes away when the person stops being an admin and does not come back with the role. A line `newsletter.manager_granted` / `newsletter.manager_revoked` goes to the admin log only when the value really changed.

- **The field `canManageNewsletters`** (boolean) is next to `role` in the login, the registration, `GET` and `PATCH /api/users/me`, `POST /api/root/admins` and the list of admins: always `true` for root, the right root gave for an admin, `false` for a user. The page shows the section of the newsletters by it.
- **The frontend may keep the value** (in memory, `localStorage` or a cookie) **but must read it again from `GET /api/users/me` at every opening of the app**; a stale `true` only shows a button that leads to a `403`.
- **The server never trusts it.** Every route of the people who run the newsletters asks the database at every request (`requireNewsletterManager`, after `authGuard` and the role): an admin without the right gets `403 NEWSLETTER_MANAGER_REQUIRED`, a user `403 INSUFFICIENT_ROLE`. `409 USER_NOT_ADMIN` when root gives the right to an account that is not an admin; `404 USER_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown account or a deleted admin that would be given it.

All the managers see and change all the campaigns. Every route below is for them (root and an admin with the right).

### Campaigns

| Route                                           | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `POST /api/newsletters/campaigns`               | a draft: `{ name (1-200 chars, one line), missingLocale?, content? }` -> `201`                                                                                                                                                        |

| `PATCH /api/newsletters/campaigns/:campaignId`  | any of `name`, `missingLocale`, `content` (the WHOLE content is replaced, not merged); only a draft or a scheduled campaign                                                                                                           |
| `DELETE /api/newsletters/campaigns/:campaignId` | a draft only                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `POST .../copy`                                 | a new draft "Copy of ..." with the rule and the content of a campaign of any state (the data of the products is taken again when it starts)                                                                                           |
| `POST .../schedule`                             | exactly one of `{ scheduledAt }` and `{ runNow: true }` (below)                                                                                                                                                                       |
| `POST .../cancel`, `POST .../stop`              | call off a scheduled campaign; stop one that is going out                                                                                                                                                                             |
| `POST .../preview`, `POST .../test`             | the mail as a reader gets it; the same mail sent to an admin (below)                                                                                                                                                                  |

Refusals: `404 NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_FOUND`; `409 NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_EDITABLE` (also when the system started the campaign between the read and the write), `NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_DELETABLE`, `NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`, `NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_CANCELLABLE`, `NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_STOPPABLE`. The order of the checks is always: the body (`400 VALIDATION_ERROR`), the rules of the content (`400`: `LOCALE_INVALID`, `NEWSLETTER_LOCALE_WITHOUT_SUBJECT`, `NEWSLETTER_LINK_NOT_ALLOWED`, `NEWSLETTER_ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED`), the campaign (`404`), the state (`409`). Every change writes a line to the admin log; the lines never hold a text or an address.

**The content.** A language is in the campaign when it has a subject; the blocks are ONE list for all languages and each text inside a block is a map of the languages.

```json
{
  "subject": { "en": "New in the shop", "uk": "Новинки магазину" },
  "preview": { "en": "Fresh from the warehouse" },
  "blocks": [
    { "type": "text", "text": { "en": "# Autumn\n\nSee **what is new**: [the shop](/products)" } },
    {
      "type": "products",
      "source": "rule",
      "rule": "new",
      "limit": 4,
      "title": { "en": "New" }
    },
    { "type": "products", "source": "manual", "productIds": [12, 15] },
    { "type": "category", "categoryId": 3, "title": { "en": "Shirts" } },
    { "type": "button", "kind": "sale", "url": "/sale" }
  ]
}
```

| Part       | Rule                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `subject`  | required, one line of 1 to 150 characters for each language; `preview` is the line under it (0 to 150), optional                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| languages  | the key of each map is exactly the code of a language the shop answers in now (`es`, not `ES` or `es-ES`): `400 LOCALE_INVALID` with `details.field` and `details.locale`; a text, preview or title of a language with no subject is `400 NEWSLETTER_LOCALE_WITHOUT_SUBJECT`, so the page sends the subject first |
| blocks     | up to 10: `text`, `products`, `category`, `button`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `text`     | up to 5000 characters for each language, in the small markup below                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `products` | by hand (`productIds`, 1 to 12 different ids, the order kept) or by a rule (`new`, `sale`, `popular`, `bestsellers` with `periodDays` 7, 30 or 90, `top_rated`), with `limit` 1 to 12 (4 unless given) and `categoryId` if wanted; a `title` of up to 120 characters                                              |
| `category` | `categoryId` and an optional `title`                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `button`   | `kind` (`shop`, `sale`, `new`, `category`, `product`) and `url` of 1 to 2000 characters; the label comes from the dictionary of the language of the reader                                                                                                                                                        |
| size       | the body of a request is up to 1 MB (`413` above it); a mail over 100 KB of html is cut by Gmail, which hides the unsubscribe: the preview warns `MAIL_TOO_LARGE`                                                                                                                                                 |

The body is checked **strictly**: no type is turned into another (`"5"` is not `5`) and a field the schema does not name is refused with `400 VALIDATION_ERROR`, not dropped. A draft may be incomplete; whether it is whole is judged when it is scheduled.

**The markup of a text** (the page of the form explains it, no html is accepted and a `<` stays a `<`): `**bold**`, `*italic*`, a line that starts with `# ` is a heading, lines that start with `- ` are a list, `[label](address)` is a link, an empty line starts a paragraph. The server makes a safe html and a text version of it.

**Links go to the shop, or to a trusted site.** Every link of a text and every address of a button must be an address of `WEB_ORIGIN`, a path that starts with one slash (`/sale`), or an absolute `http` or `https` address of a **trusted domain** (`newsletterTrustedDomains` of the [shop settings](shop-settings.md)) or of a subdomain of one, with no user name; any other (another host, a trusted domain as a user name or under another scheme, `javascript:`, `mailto:`, a relative path, a path with two slashes) is `400 NEWSLETTER_LINK_NOT_ALLOWED` with the `field` and the `url`. Stored links are judged again when the mail is built, by `WEB_ORIGIN` and the trusted domains as they are then: a link that is not allowed any more is written as its words and a button is left out, with the warning `LINK_NOT_ALLOWED`. Every link to the shop gets `utm_source=newsletter&utm_campaign=<id>`, so the analytics of the site sees who came from a newsletter; a link to a trusted site goes as it is, with no marks.

**Addresses written out in a text.** A mail program makes a link of an address written as plain text, so the text of the mail (the subject, the preview, a title, the text of a block) may not name another site either. An address is read in four forms: with a scheme (`http://…`, `https://…`, any `scheme://…`), with `www.` (`www.example.com`), a domain with a path (`example.com/page`, also in the letters of another alphabet or as punycode, with a port), and a name with no path, or with a `?` or a `#` right after it (`example.com`, `example.com?x=1`, `example.com#top`) **when its last label is a real top-level domain** (the list of the npm package `tlds`, which follows the root zone of IANA). Its host must be the domain of the shop (`SITE_DOMAIN`), the host of `WEB_ORIGIN`, or a **trusted domain** that root wrote in the shop settings (`newsletterTrustedDomains`, [shop settings](shop-settings.md)), or a subdomain of one of them (`cdn.shop.example` passes for `shop.example`; `evilshop.example` and `shop.example.evil.test` do not). Any other is `400 NEWSLETTER_ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED` with `details.field` and `details.address` (up to 200 characters), so the page can say "remove the address, or ask root to add its domain to the trusted ones". A text of a block is read twice, as typed and as the reader gets it (the plain text of the mail): `ev**il**.com/deal` is `evil.com/deal` to the reader, and a mail program makes a link of it, so it is refused as well (a subject, a preview and a title are plain lines: their markers are only signs). An address of mail (`a@b.test`: what stands after an `@` is a mailbox, not a site) and a name whose last word is not a zone (`e.g.`, `Mr.Smith`, `v1.2`, `photo.jpg`, `3.5`) are not read. The list alone decides, so a name that ends like a zone is read even when it is meant as a file or as a full stop with no space after it: `file.zip`, `readme.md`, `script.sh` and `done.Next` are refused (`.zip`, `.md`, `.sh` and `.next` are real zones). An address is read wherever it stands, also right after a colon (`site:example.com`, `see:example.com/page`, `x:example.com?a=1`, `Visit:www.example.com`): the colon covers nothing, in any of the four forms, and the same list of zones decides for a name with no path (`10:30`, `3:2`, `Note:see`, `Version:v1.2` and `Open:photo.jpg` are quiet; `File:readme.md` is not). A name written right after a slash, a dot, a dash or a letter is the middle of another word or of another address and is not read as one of its own. The trusted domains are read at every check (root changes them while the server runs), but only when the text is saved, scheduled and translated: **a domain that root takes off the list after a campaign is scheduled stays in it**. A trusted domain is allowed in the links of the markup and in the buttons as well (above).

**Scheduling.** `POST .../schedule` takes `{ scheduledAt }` (ISO 8601: with an offset it is that exact instant; without one it is read in the time zone of the shop, `SHOP_TIME_ZONE`, so a `datetime-local` field can be sent as it is) or `{ runNow: true }` (the time is the moment of the request, and the next pass starts it). It schedules a draft or moves the time of a scheduled campaign. The refusals come in this order: the body, the campaign (`404`), the state (`409 NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`), `400 NEWSLETTER_SCHEDULE_IN_PAST` (never for `runNow`), the content (`400 LOCALE_INVALID`, `NEWSLETTER_LOCALE_WITHOUT_SUBJECT`, `NEWSLETTER_LINK_NOT_ALLOWED`, `NEWSLETTER_ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED`, then `400 NEWSLETTER_CONTENT_INCOMPLETE`), and the limit. The content is whole when it has at least one block, the subject of the language of the shop, in that language the text of every text block and the title of every block that has a title in any language, and the same for every other language that has a subject; `details.missing` lists `{ field, locale }` of what to add, so the page can point at it. A preview line is not needed.

**The limit of the shop.** At most `newsletterLimitCount` campaigns in any window of `newsletterLimitDays` days (2 in 7 unless root set others, [shop settings](shop-settings.md)). A scheduled campaign counts at its time; one that is going out, finished or stopped counts at the time it began; a draft and a cancelled campaign count never. The window is half open and `days x 24` hours long. A request that would put more than the count in any window that holds its time is `409 NEWSLETTER_LIMIT_REACHED`; `details.campaigns` lists `{ id, name, at }` of every campaign that fills the window and `details.limit` is `{ count, days }`, so the page can offer to cancel or stop one of them or to move the new one. The check and the write run under one lock, so two managers cannot both take the last place. **The limit is checked when a campaign is scheduled or its time moved, and asked again when the system starts the campaign**, with the limit as root has set it by then (it may have been lowered since): the start takes the same lock, counts the campaigns that have gone out already (those still waiting are not counted, so the first in line is not refused for the sake of one behind it; each is judged when its turn comes), and a campaign that would break the limit is not started: it becomes `cancelled` with `cancelReason: 'limit'` (a campaign a manager called off has `cancelReason: null`), the log of the pass says so, and the alert `newsletter_cancelled_by_limit` shows it to the managers. A campaign that was cancelled cannot be scheduled again (`409 NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_NOT_SCHEDULABLE`): the manager makes a copy (`POST .../copy`, a draft whatever the state of the original) and schedules the copy for a time that fits. The list `GET /api/newsletters/campaigns?status=cancelled&cancelReason=limit&cancelledFrom=<time>` is the list of such campaigns.

**Preview.** `POST .../preview` with `{ locale }` gives `{ subject, preheader, html, text, warnings, takenAt }`: the mail as a reader of that language gets it (the logo on top, as a link to the file at Cloudinary (`logoUrl` of the [shop settings](shop-settings.md)), never an attachment; the blocks, no greeting, and the foot every mail has: the seller, why the reader got it, "Manage the subscription", that the mail is automatic and where to write, all in the language of the reader). Nothing is sent or written; a campaign in any state can be looked at (and the preview has no mark of a test: it is the mail of a subscriber). The products are taken from the catalog at the moment of the request, the list a buyer sees. A card of a product on sale shows the sale price, the regular price struck out, the lowest price of the 30 days before the sale, and **"Sale until {date}"** (the day the sale ends, in the time zone of the shop, written in the language of the reader) when the sale has an end. The sale is judged at the moment the mail is built, not when the products were taken: a sale that ended meanwhile (`saleEndsAt` is not after that moment) is shown as a card with the regular price, with no "until" and no lowest-price line. What cannot be shown is left out and told in `warnings` (`{ blockIndex, code, productId? }`): `PRODUCT_UNAVAILABLE`, `BLOCK_EMPTY`, `CATEGORY_MISSING`, `LINK_NOT_ALLOWED`, `TEXT_MISSING` (no text in this language: the block is left out, the language of the shop is not put in its place), `TITLE_MISSING`, `MAIL_EMPTY` (no block is left), `MAIL_TOO_LARGE`. Its links of the subscription lead to the home page. **Show `html` in an `iframe` with `sandbox` only.** `400 LOCALE_INVALID` for a language that is not on now; `400 NEWSLETTER_LOCALE_NOT_IN_CAMPAIGN` for one the campaign has no subject in.

**Test mail.** `POST .../test` with `{ email, locale }` sends the same mail for real to an address of an account of an admin or of root that was not deleted (the own one or a colleague's). Any other address, an unknown one included, gets the same `403 NEWSLETTER_TEST_RECIPIENT_NOT_ALLOWED`. **It is marked as a test**: the subject starts with "[Test]" (`[Тест]`, `[Prueba]`, in the language of the mail) and the first line of the mail says "This is a test mail; the subscribers did not get it." (in the text version as well). A user may send `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_TEST_PER_USER` test mails an hour in all campaigns together (30 unless set; `429 RATE_LIMITED` with `details.limit` `newsletterTest`), with no wait between two. Its links of the subscription carry a token no subscription has: the page says the subscription was not found and the unsubscribe of the mail program does nothing. The admin log gets `newsletter.campaign_test_sent` with the campaign and the account of the recipient, never the address. `502 NEWSLETTER_TEST_SEND_FAILED` when the provider does not take it.

**Translation.** `POST /api/newsletters/translate` with `{ from?, to, items }` gives drafts of the texts in other languages through OpenAI and saves nothing: `to` 1 to 20 languages (each one the shop answers in), `items` 1 to 30 texts `{ key, text, kind ('line' or 'markup'), maxChars? }`, 20000 characters in all (`400 NEWSLETTER_TRANSLATE_TOO_MUCH_TEXT`). The answer is `{ translations: { <locale>: { <key>: text } }, warnings: [{ locale, key, code, field?, address? }] }`. The page shows the drafts for a person to edit as plain text and saves the languages they keep with `PATCH`. A draft the server does not trust is left out and named in `warnings`: `MISSING`, `TOO_LONG`, `NOT_A_LINE`, `HTML_NOT_ALLOWED`, `LINK_CHANGED` (an address is never translated, added or dropped), `ADDRESS_NOT_ALLOWED` (the draft names a site that is neither the shop's nor a trusted domain: the same rule the content is saved under, so a draft that is given back can be saved; only this warning has `field`, the `key` and the language as `<key>.<locale>`, the place the content error names when the draft is saved under that key, and `address`, the first address that is not allowed, up to 200 characters); a language that could not be asked comes empty with `TRANSLATION_FAILED`, and when none could be asked, or `OPENAI_API_KEY` is not set, the answer is `502 NEWSLETTER_TRANSLATE_FAILED`. A request may take up to about 90 seconds (block the button and show the wait) and costs money: send it only from a button. A user may ask `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_TRANSLATE_PER_USER` times an hour (20 unless set; `429 RATE_LIMITED`). Texts are sent to OpenAI without storing (`store: false`) and are never logged. **The privacy policy is not changed for it**: the text of a newsletter is not personal data. The page says so to the person before the request ("the texts go to OpenAI, do not put personal data in them"), because the shop cannot tell what a person types.

**A language that is turned off.** `disabledLocales` in the campaign (and in every row of the list) names the languages that have a subject in the campaign and that the shop does not answer in any more; the form marks them "language turned off". The text stays, can be edited or dropped (the whole content sent with `PATCH` may keep it), but a text in a language that is off that the campaign did not already have is `400 LOCALE_INVALID`. Scheduling asks nothing of the text of a language that is off. A preview and a test mail in it are refused.

### The subscribers and the blocklist

**The managers see the email addresses of the subscribers.** The privacy policy of the shop has to say so (see "The terms and the privacy policy").

| Route                                               | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GET /api/newsletters/subscribers/summary`          | the numbers: `active`, `byLocale` (`locale: null` is the bucket of no language, shown as "shop language"), `bySource` (`form`, `account`), `pending`, `pausedComplaint`, `stoppedBounce`                                                                                               |
| `GET /api/newsletters/subscribers`                  | the list, every state, newest first, a cursor and `total`; filters `status`, `source`, `locale` (`none` for no language) through commas and `email` (a part of the address, any case); `400 CURSOR_INVALID`                                                                            |
| `DELETE /api/newsletters/subscribers/:subscriberId` | erases an address on the word of the person (the request comes through support): every subscription of the address is made anonymous in place (below); no mail is sent; `{ erased: true, subscriptions }`, `404 NEWSLETTER_SUBSCRIBER_NOT_FOUND` for an unknown id and the second time |
| `GET /api/newsletters/blocklist?email=`             | `{ blocked, entry }`: the entry is the id, the date, the admin who blocked it or the person herself (`byPerson`), the reason; never a hash                                                                                                                                             |
| `POST /api/newsletters/blocklist`                   | `{ email, reason? }` blocks an address (`201` for an entry made now, `200` with the first entry when it was blocked already; `400 INVALID_EMAIL`); a request of that address that waits for its confirmation is removed, a subscription in force stays                                 |

A row of the list has the id, the address, the source, the language, the state, the account (`userId`), the dates and the reason it ended, and never a token, a hash or a salt. The blocklist keeps a keyed hash of the address and no address, so an admin finds an entry by typing the address and cannot read the list. The admin log gets `newsletter.subscriber_erased`, `newsletter.block_added` and `newsletter.block_removed` by the ids, never the address.

**What the erasure does.** The id names any subscription of the address; the erasure takes every subscription the address ever had (any state, the form's and the account's), in one transaction, and `subscriptions` in the answer is how many rows it made anonymous. A row that is anonymous already is not done again, so the second call is `404`. For each row:

| Field                                                                          | After the erasure                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `email`                                                                        | `deleted+<id of the row>@deleted.invalid` (`.invalid` is reserved and is never delivered)                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `emailHmac` (the keyed hash of the address), `confirmHash`, `confirmExpiresAt` | emptied                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `endedUserId` (the account whose deletion ended the row)                       | emptied                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `userId` of a subscription of an account (`source` `account`)                  | kept: the account itself holds the address, so the row stays tied to it; the deletion of the account later finds nothing in force on the row (it is `ended`) and leaves it as the erasure made it, and `cleanup_newsletter_consent()` removes it with its journal once its term has passed |
| `manageSalt`, `manageHash`, `unsubscribeHash`                                  | new random values nobody holds, so no link of any mail the address was sent opens anything                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `status`, `endReason`, `endedAt`                                               | a row that was live (`pending`, `active`, `paused_complaint`) ends: `ended`, `removed_by_admin`, now; a row that was `ended` or `stopped_bounce` keeps all three                                                                                                                           |
| `ip` and `userAgent` of every line of its journal                              | emptied (the lines stay)                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |

What **stays**: the row (its id, source, language, dates and the `userId` of a subscription of an account), the lines of the journal (the event, the place, the time, the language, the version of the privacy policy, the campaign) as the proof that a consent was given, the mails of the campaigns (`newsletter_recipients`: a mail still waiting for a subscription that ended is left out by the sending) and the totals of the campaigns, the blocklist (it holds the hash of an address that asked for no more mail, which still should not get any), and the accounts. An erased subscription of an account (`userId`) is still in the export of that account, without the IP address and the browser of its lines; an erased subscription of the form has no address left to be found by, so no export lists it.

## Sending

**The pass.** A campaign is sent by a pass: a command that starts the scheduled campaigns whose time has come and sends their mails, then exits.

```bash
npm run build
npm run newsletter:run
```

It needs the same environment as the server and a reachable Redis (it holds a lock `lock:newsletters:pass` there, so two passes never run at once; one that finds the lock held ends at once). The exit code is `0` when the pass ran (also when it found the lock held), `1` when it could not do its work: Redis or the database are out of reach, or SendGrid refused the key or the stream has another problem the pass stopped on (the log says which). A pass takes at most 4 minutes. **Until the service of periodic tasks exists nothing runs it: run it by hand, and mind where**: it sends real mails with the key of the newsletters to the subscribers of whatever database its environment points at. The service is to run it every 5 minutes; a skipped run harms nothing. In the log the line `newsletter pass finished` carries the counters (`launched`, `cancelled`, `claimed`, `sent`, `rejected`, `retried`, `skipped`, `released`, `deferred`, `finished`) and the reason it ended (`done`, `daily_limit`, `deadline`, `halted`, `locked`). `cancelled` is the campaigns the limit of the shop left no room for (below); `claimed` the mails taken in portions (a mail taken twice by one pass counts twice); `sent` the mails the provider took; `rejected` the mails the provider will not take, or whose tries are used up, that the pass judged (a mail the database put away before the claim, after its last lease, is counted in the campaign but not here); `retried` the mails that found the provider busy and wait out their lease; `skipped` the mails left out (the subscription ended meanwhile, or nothing was left in the mail); `released` the mails given back to the queue with their try not counted, free at once (not tried: a campaign stopped meanwhile, or the rest of a portion when the pass stopped; or tried and found the provider busy, in a stopped pass); `deferred` the mails the provider refused in a run of refusals that stopped the pass, which keep the try they used and wait out their lease (see "Retries, refusals and stops" below).

**What a pass does.**

1. **Starts** the scheduled campaigns whose time has come (oldest first). **The limit of the campaigns is asked again for each** (see "The limit of the shop"): one that does not fit is `cancelled` with the reason `limit`, not started; one that cannot be started for another reason stays `scheduled` and the next pass tries again. The products of the blocks are taken **once**, at that moment, so everyone gets the same mail even if the sending takes a day: a product that left the catalog or sold out is left out, and a block that stays empty does not go. One row of the mails is made for every subscription **in force at that moment** (`active`), with the language fixed for it: the language of the subscription; for one with no language the language of the shop if the campaign has a subject in it; a subscriber whose language has no text gets the language of the shop (`missingLocale: shop_locale`) or nothing (`skip`). Whoever subscribes later does not get this campaign, and whoever unsubscribes before their mail is built does not either.
2. **Sends** in portions of 50, five at a time, within `NEWSLETTER_DAILY_LIMIT` mails of the newsletters for one day of the shop. **The queue goes by the tries a mail has used, the fewest first, and by id (the order of the subscriptions at the start) among those with the same**, both in the choice of the portion and in the order inside it: the mails never tried come before a mail tried once, and that one before a mail tried twice. So a mail that was refused, or found the provider busy, comes back behind all the mails that have not been tried yet, and a few mails the provider refuses for themselves cannot keep the rest of a campaign from going. The database keeps an index over the mails that wait for this order (`newsletter_recipients_queue_idx`: the tries, then the id). The plan of SendGrid is shared with the mails of the orders, so set the limit with room for them. **What does not fit goes the next day**; the campaign stays `sending` until its last mail is answered for.
3. **Looks at the subscription again before every mail**: a campaign stopped, a subscription ended, paused or stopped meanwhile means no mail (`skipped`). No subscriber gets more than one mail of a campaign.
4. **Retries, refusals and stops.**
   - **The provider is busy.** A temporary failure (`429`, `5xx`, a timeout, no answer) leaves the mail queued under its lease: it is taken again not earlier than `NEWSLETTER_RETRY_MINUTES` later (10 unless set; a pass that dies on a mail frees it after the same time), and when it has failed `NEWSLETTER_MAX_ATTEMPTS` times (5 unless set) the last failure makes it `rejected`. A mail whose tries are used up and whose last lease ran out without an answer (the process died on it) is made `rejected` by the next claim and counted in the campaign.
   - **The provider refuses the mail.** A refusal that is the fault of the mail (most `4xx`) makes it `rejected`, **but not at once: the refused mail is held** until the pass learns whether the fault is the mail's or the stream's. A mail the provider takes ends the run of refusals before it, and the held mails are then rejected (each was refused one by one, so the fault was its own); a pass that ends without a long run judges what it holds the same way.
   - **A mail tried before and refused again.** A mail that is not on its first try and is refused again, when the campaign had a mail taken by the provider after the previous try of this mail (also one taken earlier in the same pass), is `rejected` **at once** and does not enter the run of refusals: it does not count toward the ten. The stream took mails after that try, so the fault is the mail's. When no mail of the campaign was taken since its previous try, it enters the run like any other. A mail refused while the pass is stopping is held with the rest: a stopped pass rejects nothing. A mail given back to the queue after a busy try (in a stopped pass) is as it was before it was taken, and its previous try reads as the time it was made. "After" is read on one clock, the database's: the claim gives the time the mail was taken last, and a mail the provider took is written down with the clock of the database in its `updatedAt` at that moment (`sentAt` is the clock of the pass, for the limit of the day, and is not compared with it). A mail reported on later (a bounce) is no longer `sent` and does not count.
   - **Ten in a row stop the pass.** Ten mails in a row refused, or ten tries in a row that found the provider busy or silent, are taken for the fault of the stream, not of the mails (the same for a refusal of the key or the account, `401` and `403`, a missing setting and a mail that cannot be built). The pass stops and judges nothing: no mail is rejected for it. Each mail it holds is settled by how it failed. **A mail the provider refused keeps the try it used** and waits out its lease, so it comes back after `NEWSLETTER_RETRY_MINUTES`, behind the mails with fewer tries (`deferred` in the summary). **A mail that found the provider busy, and every mail of the portion that was not tried, goes back to the queue as it was before the pass took it**: the try is not counted and the mail is free at once, not after its lease (`released`). A mail that was already with SendGrid when the tenth refusal came may still be taken: it is written down as sent, but it judges nobody, since a stopped stream rejects nothing. The log has the line `newsletter pass stopped` with the reason (`every_mail_refused`, `provider_unavailable`, `access_refused`, `stream_broken`, `campaign_data_missing`, `database_failed`).
   - **What this means for a campaign.** If the first mails of the queue are refused for themselves (a broken text in one language, say), the pass stops on them once, the next pass sends all the mails not tried, and the ones that were refused are tried again after their lease, behind everything else: the campaign had mails taken since their previous try, so each is rejected at once, and the pass does not stop again. If the provider refuses every mail (a fault of ours), each stopped pass spends one try of about ten mails and of no other, and the passes take the mails with the fewest tries first, so the mails are tried round after round, ten at a time. **Nothing is rejected before its tries are used up, and a fix deployed meanwhile sends everything; but a campaign small enough wears out**: after `NEWSLETTER_MAX_ATTEMPTS` rounds its mails are rejected (the claim puts them away once the lease of the last try has run out). That cost is accepted; the stop writes the alert "the sending worker stopped" so it is seen.
   - **A mail the provider took is never given back by the pass**, whatever fails after it: its row is written down first, and only then are the refusals held before it judged; if the database fails on that write the row stays under its lease and is tried again only when the lease has run out (see "Known limitations"). A mail that is taken again by the same pass (a short `NEWSLETTER_RETRY_MINUTES` lets its lease run out within a pass) is judged by its last try and counted once.
   - **The mark of a stop.** A stop writes a mark in Redis for the alert "the sending worker stopped" (below), and the next pass that gets a mail taken by the provider, or finds no mail of a sending campaign left in the queue (waiting or leased), takes the mark away. A pass that ends because the limit of the day is spent (`daily_limit`) or its time is up (`deadline`), or that sent nothing while mails still wait (the claim came back empty only because they are leased to a try that is not over, for example when SendGrid is busy again), has proved nothing about the stream and leaves the mark as it is.
5. **Finishes** a campaign that has no mail left in the queue (`finished`); a stopped one never finishes.

Every mail of the stream (the mails of a campaign and the two mails of the subscription) goes from the address `NEWSLETTER_EMAIL_FROM` under a **name** that is, in this order, the first that is not empty: `newsletterFromName`, `emailFromName`, `shopName` ([shop settings](shop-settings.md); read at every mail, so root changes it while the server runs; the mails of the orders keep `emailFromName`, then `shopName`). No `Reply-To` (answers go nowhere), the category `newsletter`, the two unsubscribe headers, and the custom arguments of the webhook; the tracking of clicks, opens and the subscription is off on the mail itself.

**The totals of a campaign** (`counters`): `planned` (the mails to send), `sent` (the provider took them), `rejected` (it did not), `undelivered` (bounces and drops reported later), `complaints`, `unsubscribed` (from the mail program or the page, counted against the campaign the mail came from: only a campaign that has a mail to that subscription counts it). Opens and clicks are not counted.

**A campaign being stopped.** `POST .../stop` ends a campaign that is going out: whoever has not been sent the mail yet will not be, the mails already sent are not recalled. The pass looks at the state before every mail, so the stop works on the next mail, not the next portion.

## The alerts and the journals

Seven kinds of the alerts of the admins are for the managers only (root, and an admin with the right, asked of the database at every request). Any other admin gets `404 ALERT_KIND_NOT_FOUND` for them, and an admin who loses the right stops seeing, counting and setting them at once. Each admin follows the kinds they want and sets their own threshold ([API](api.md) and Swagger: `/api/admin/alerts`).

| Kind                            | Case is    | Counts                                                                                                                                                  | Threshold (default, bounds)       |
| ------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `newsletter_undelivered`        | a campaign | the mails that did not arrive are over this share of the mails sent                                                                                     | `%`, 5, 1 to 100                  |
| `newsletter_rejected`           | a campaign | the mails the provider did not take are over this share of the mails answered                                                                           | `%`, 5, 1 to 100                  |
| `newsletter_complaints`         | a campaign | the complaints are over this share of the mails sent                                                                                                    | **`‰` (per mille)**, 3, 1 to 1000 |
| `newsletter_running_long`       | a campaign | a campaign is going out for more days than this                                                                                                         | days, 3, 1 to 365                 |
| `newsletter_starting_soon`      | a campaign | a campaign is scheduled to start within this many hours, or its time has passed and nothing started it                                                  | hours, 24, 1 to 720               |
| `newsletter_cancelled_by_limit` | a campaign | the campaigns the limit of the shop left no room for when their time came (`cancelled`, `cancelReason: 'limit'`), called off in the last this many days | days, 7, 1 to 365                 |
| `newsletter_sending_halted`     | one state  | the sending worker stopped on a problem of the stream and no pass has gone through since (count 0 or 1)                                                 | none                              |

**The complaint threshold is in thousandths, not in per cent.** A complaint is rare (the mail providers judge a sender by a few in a thousand, and a whole per cent would be far too coarse), so `newsletter_complaints` and the filter `complaintsOver` take a whole number of **mails in a thousand (‰)**: the unit of the threshold in `GET /api/admin/alerts` is `permille` and the page labels it "per thousand". `3` is 3 in 1000, that is **0.3 %**. Example: a campaign with 5000 mails taken by the provider is over a threshold of 3 from the 16th complaint (16 in 5000 is 3.2 ‰); 15 complaints is exactly 3 ‰ and is not over it (the share must be **more** than the threshold, compared in whole numbers). The other two shares stay in per cent (`undeliveredOver`, `rejectedOver`, 1 to 100).

The shares are judged on the campaigns that are going out, finished or stopped and began in the last 14 days (bounces and complaints keep coming for days, and an alert that can never go out is noise), and only from 20 mails answered for (one mail of two tells nothing). The list of every case is `GET /api/newsletters/campaigns` with the filters `startedFrom`, `startedTo`, `scheduledTo`, `cancelledFrom`, `cancelReason`, `undeliveredOver`, `rejectedOver`, `complaintsOver`, so the count of an alert is the `total` of its list.

**The stop of the sending worker** is one state, not a list: no threshold, no list (`list: null`), no case to put aside. `GET /api/admin/alerts` gives every alert a field `occurredAt`: the time of the last stop for this kind (`null` for the others, and for this one while there was no stop). The stop is written by the pass (a mark in Redis, 30 days at most) when it stops on a problem of the stream (the key of SendGrid refused, a setting missing, ten refusals or ten busy answers in a row); the next pass that gets a mail taken, or finds no mail of a sending campaign left in the queue (waiting or leased), takes the mark away and the alert goes out; a pass that sent nothing while mails still wait leaves it. With Redis out of reach the alert shows 0.

The admin log (`GET /api/admin/audit-log`) has 13 events of the newsletters, filtered by `events`, `actions` or `entityType`: `newsletter.manager_granted`, `newsletter.manager_revoked`, `newsletter.campaign_created`, `_updated`, `_deleted`, `_copied`, `_scheduled`, `_cancelled`, `_stopped`, `_test_sent`, `newsletter.subscriber_erased`, `newsletter.block_added`, `newsletter.block_removed` (kinds of thing `newsletter_campaign`, `newsletter_subscriber`, `newsletter_block`, actions `NEWSLETTER_MANAGER_CHANGE`, `NEWSLETTER_CAMPAIGN_CHANGE`, `NEWSLETTER_SUBSCRIBER_DELETE`, `NEWSLETTER_BLOCK_CHANGE`; the erasure keeps the action name of the removal it replaced). They are about what people do. The system's own steps (the start, the end of a campaign, a campaign called off by the limit, the events of the webhook) have no author and are seen in the totals of the campaign and in the journal of the subscription.

## Turning a language off

Root takes a language out of `enabledLocales` with `PUT /api/root/shop-settings` (or `.../enabledLocales`). In the same transaction every account and every subscription that is not ended and has that language is left with **no language** (they are mailed in the language of the shop from then on); turning the language on again brings no one back and sends no mail. See [languages](languages.md).

**The frontend must warn root before the write.** `GET /api/root/shop-settings/locale-counts` gives `{ items: [{ locale, accounts, subscriptions }] }` (root only). For each language the new set leaves out, the page shows something like "1204 accounts and 760 subscriptions will move to the language of the shop" and asks to confirm. The answer of the `PUT` does not carry the numbers.

## The terms and the privacy policy

Two terms of the privacy policy belong to the newsletters; root sets both in days when publishing a version (`POST /api/root/legal-documents`, kind `privacy`, `retention`), every term from 1 to 36500 days, all eight terms of the policy together:

| Term (`retention.*`)       | Data                                                                                                 | Counted from                |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| `newsletterConsentDays`    | the proof of the consent: an ended (or bounced) subscription together with the journal of its events | the end of the subscription |
| `newsletterRecipientsDays` | the mails of a campaign: a row for each subscriber it went to                                        | the end of the campaign     |

A request to subscribe that nobody confirmed goes after the 3 days of its link, with no term in the policy. A piece of data is kept for the shorter of the term of the version in force when it was collected and of the version in force now, and is cleaned a day early; without a published policy nothing is cleaned. Three database functions do it, each as the system and without a parameter: `cleanup_newsletter_pending()`, `cleanup_newsletter_consent()` and `cleanup_newsletter_recipients()`, next to the six of the other data ([Returns](returns.md) has the table). **Nothing calls any of the nine yet** (see "Known limitations").

A database that already holds a version of the policy from before the newsletters has six terms and is refused by the check of the table: those versions have to be removed before the scripts are applied, and root publishes the policy again with all eight terms.

**What the text of the policy must say about the newsletters.** The terms are fields of the version; the text is root's and has to say four things:

1. the admins who run the newsletters (root and the admins root allowed) see the email addresses of the subscribers;
2. the blocklist of the form keeps only a keyed hash of each address, with **no term**, until an admin removes the entry;
3. the proof of the consent (when, the address and the browser, the version of the policy) is kept for the first term after the subscription ended;
4. the mails of a campaign are kept for the second term after the campaign ended.

## What the owner sets up

**Two mail streams.** The mails of the orders (`SENDGRID_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM`) and the newsletters (`SENDGRID_NEWSLETTER_API_KEY`, `NEWSLETTER_EMAIL_FROM`) must be two streams, each with its own lists of complaints, bounces and unsubscribes, so a complaint about a newsletter never blocks a receipt: in production two SendGrid accounts or two subaccounts. One account for both is for development only, and its lists overlap. The newsletters are sent from a subdomain of their own (for example `news@mail.example.com`) with its own domain authentication, so complaints touch the reputation of the orders' domain less.

**Environment** (every key is in [configuration](configuration.md) and `.env.example`; values are never written in a file):

| Key                                                                                           | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `SENDGRID_NEWSLETTER_API_KEY`                                                                 | the key of the SendGrid account of the newsletters (rights: Mail Send and Suppressions)                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `SENDGRID_NEWSLETTER_WEBHOOK_KEY`                                                             | the Verification Key of the Event Webhook of that account (base64 of a DER public key of P-256; **each webhook has its own**)                                                                                                                                    |
| `NEWSLETTER_EMAIL_FROM`                                                                       | the sender, a bare address of the authenticated domain                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
| `NEWSLETTER_BLOCK_SECRET`, `NEWSLETTER_LINK_SECRET`                                           | two secrets of 32 bytes or more (base64 or base64url, `openssl rand -base64 48`), **different from each other and between the environments, never changed after the launch** (the blocklist would stop blocking; the links in the sent mails would stop working) |
| `NEWSLETTER_DAILY_LIMIT`                                                                      | the mails of the newsletters one day, under the plan of SendGrid                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`                                                                        | the secret of the Cloudflare Turnstile widget                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| `NEWSLETTER_RETRY_MINUTES`                                                                    | the minutes a mail the provider did not take (busy, no answer, or refused in a run of refusals that stopped the pass) waits before it is tried again, and how long the pass that took it keeps it: 10 unless set, a whole number from 1 to 1440 (the database keeps the same bounds)                                                         |
| `NEWSLETTER_MAX_ATTEMPTS`                                                                     | how many times a mail is tried before it is given up and counted as rejected: 5 unless set, a whole number from 1 to 20                                                                                                                                          |
| `API_ORIGIN`                                                                                  | the address of the API from outside (https in production, no path): the unsubscribe header names it; **required** like the keys above, outside the tests                                                                                                         |
| `WEB_PAGE_NEWSLETTER_CONFIRM`, `_DECLINE`, `_MANAGE`, `WEB_PAGE_PRODUCT`, `WEB_PAGE_CATEGORY` | the pages above (the defaults in the table are the contract with the frontend; no `#` in the value of a page that carries a token)                                                                                                                               |
| `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_LINK_PER_ADDRESS`                                                           | the requests from one address to the pages behind the links in a fixed window of 10 minutes (60 unless set)                                                                                                                                                      |
| `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_UNSUBSCRIBE_PER_ADDRESS`                                                    | the requests from one address to the unsubscribe button of the mails (the `POST` and the `GET`, one count of its own) in a fixed window of 10 minutes (1000 unless set)                                                                                          |
| `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_TRANSLATE_PER_USER`                                                         | translations a manager asks for an hour (20 unless set); the translation also needs `OPENAI_API_KEY`                                                                                                                                                             |
| `LIMIT_NEWSLETTER_TEST_PER_USER`                                                              | test mails a manager sends an hour, in all campaigns together (30 unless set)                                                                                                                                                                                    |

The seven keys from `SENDGRID_NEWSLETTER_API_KEY` to `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY` and `API_ORIGIN` are required: the start stops without them in development and in production (a value of spaces only is missing too), with a message that names the key and the rule and never the value. The server also stops if the two secrets are the same or shorter than 32 bytes, if the key of the webhook is not a P-256 public key, if `NEWSLETTER_RETRY_MINUTES` or `NEWSLETTER_MAX_ATTEMPTS` is out of its bounds, or if the value of a `WEB_PAGE_*` page that carries a token holds a `#`. A "fixed window" starts with the first request of an address and ends 10 minutes later, then the count starts over. The limits of the campaigns (`newsletterLimitCount`, `newsletterLimitDays`) and the name and the trusted domains of the newsletters (`newsletterFromName`, `newsletterTrustedDomains`) are data of the shop that root changes without a deploy ([shop settings](shop-settings.md)).

**SendGrid.** Authenticate the domain of the sender; make a key for the newsletters; make an Event Webhook of that account with the address `https://<api-domain>/api/newsletters/sendgrid/webhook`, the events **Dropped, Bounced and Spam Reports**, and **Signed event** on; copy its Verification Key into `SENDGRID_NEWSLETTER_WEBHOOK_KEY` of the matching environment. With the webhook's "Test Integration" the server answers `200 { received: true, applied: 0, skipped: N }` (the test batch carries none of our marks, so this checks the key, not the handling); `401` means the key in the environment is not the one of this webhook, `503` that it is blank or not P-256, `404` that the old code is still running. Keep click tracking, open tracking and subscription tracking off for the account (the app also turns them off on every mail): links with tokens must not pass through the servers of SendGrid, and tracking opens in the EU needs consent. Point the replies of `news@...` nowhere (for example the rule `news@... -> Drop` of Cloudflare Email Routing on the subdomain).

**Cloudflare Turnstile.** Make a widget for the hostnames of the frontend (mode Managed); the Site Key goes to the frontend, the Secret Key to `TURNSTILE_SECRET_KEY`. Until a frontend has its widget, the secret of a dev environment can be the public test secret of Cloudflare (`1x0000000000000000000000000000000AA`), which passes every check: Swagger then accepts any non-empty `captchaToken`. A server with the secret of a real widget refuses a request without a real answer.

**DNS.** The records of the domain authentication of the subdomain live next to the ones of the root domain. Tightening DMARC is not done yet: the `_dmarc` records of the root and of the mail subdomain are `p=none`; after some weeks of clean reports (only SendGrid, all passing) go to `p=quarantine`, then `p=reject`, and move the SPF of the root from `~all` to `-all` at the same time.

**The first launch.** Apply the migrations and the scripts in the order of [commands](commands.md) (with the application stopped), set the environment, publish the privacy policy with the eight terms and the four statements above, fill the data of the seller and the logo ([shop settings](shop-settings.md): the foot of every mail names the seller, and the logo is a link in every mail), optionally the name the newsletters are sent under (`newsletterFromName`) and the domains a text may name besides the shop's own (`newsletterTrustedDomains`), give the right to an admin if one besides root will run the campaigns, make the pages above on the frontend, and test one campaign on a few addresses of your own (a test mail first, then a campaign scheduled with `runNow` while only your own addresses are subscribed, then `npm run newsletter:run`).

## Known limitations and open items

- **Nothing runs the pass or the cleanups yet.** `npm run newsletter:run` is run by hand until the service of periodic tasks is built, and so are the nine cleanups of the data kept too long (they are database functions that no code calls). Until they run, no data goes by its term and the unconfirmed requests whose link expired stay (they are dead already).
- **A mail may be sent twice**: the sending is at least once, not exactly once. It happens when SendGrid took a mail but the pass never learned it: the process dies between SendGrid taking the mail and the result being written, the database fails on that write, or SendGrid answers after the time limit of the request (7 s) or with a `5xx` although it took the mail. A mail the pass knows SendGrid took is never given back to the queue: if the write fails it stays under its lease. The pass then sends the mail again after the lease (`NEWSLETTER_RETRY_MINUTES`, 10 minutes unless set) or at its next run. SendGrid has no key that would make a repeat of the same request do nothing.
- **The size of a start.** A campaign start writes the row of every subscriber in one statement; on hundreds of thousands of subscribers it can hit the time limit of the database role (15 s), and the campaign would stay `scheduled`. It would have to be cut into parts.
- **A campaign that runs late.** The system may start a campaign later than its time; it then counts in the limit of the shop by the time it began. While a start runs (a few seconds for a big audience) the steps of a person that read their subscription for update (an unsubscribe, a confirmation, a new request, the profile) wait for it.
- **A stream that refuses every mail wears a small campaign out.** Ten mails in a row refused by the provider are taken for a fault of the stream: the pass stops and the alert "the sending worker stopped" is lit. The refused mails keep their try and wait out their lease, so a few bad mails at the head of the queue do not hold the rest back. But when the provider refuses every mail (a bug of ours, a wrong text or header), each stopped pass spends one try of about ten mails, and a small campaign can use up the tries of all its mails and have them `rejected` before a fix is deployed (see "Retries, refusals and stops"). That is the price of never judging a mail by a stream that may be broken; the alert is there to be acted on.
- **A pass that could not start leaves no mark.** The alert "the sending worker stopped" is written by a pass that stopped on a problem of the stream. A pass that could not get as far as sending (Redis out of reach, or the database failing outside the sending of a mail, for example when the campaigns are looked up or a portion is taken; exit code `1`) writes nothing; it shows indirectly as a campaign still `scheduled` after its time (`newsletter_starting_soon`) or one that never finishes (`newsletter_running_long`).
- **The domains a root trusts are checked when a text is saved, scheduled and translated, not when the mail goes out**: a domain taken off the list after a campaign was scheduled stays in it. The links of the markup and the buttons are judged again when the mail is built, with the trusted domains as they are then (a domain taken off the list leaves a link as words and a button out, with a warning). A name with no path is judged by the list of top-level domains: a name whose last word is not a real zone is not read, so a manager can still name another site in words.
- **The reason `Spam Reporting Address` of a `dropped` event has not been seen in a live event.** It is matched by its text as SendGrid documents it, without regard to case, as `Bounced Address` is (that one is checked against a live event).
- **The Event Webhook** handles one event in its own transaction (about 30 ms); a batch of some hundreds of real events comes near the time SendGrid waits for an answer. It then sends the batch again, which is safe but wasted.
- **A language turned off** cannot be previewed or test-mailed until it is turned on again.
