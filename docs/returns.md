# Withdrawal and returns

This page explains how the shop takes a buyer's withdrawal from a purchase and the goods an admin takes back, to a developer who builds the frontend and to the shop owner. Every statement here is true of the code; the routes, the fields and the error codes are in [API](api.md) and in Swagger (`/documentation`), the settings are in [configuration](configuration.md). What to do step by step in each case, for the buyer, the admins and root, is in [the scenarios of returns](return-scenarios.md).

## Two rules for the frontend

- **The button of withdrawal is always there, and the server says what it does.** EU law asks for a function on the site, from 19 June 2026, with which a buyer withdraws from the contract in a few steps. While the order can be withdrawn from (`withdrawalOpen: true` in the answers about the order) the button reads "Withdraw from the contract here" and takes the statement of the buyer. The page shows `withdrawalEndsAt`, the end of the period of the contract; the shop forgives a few days more (`withdrawalGraceDays`, root sets it, never told to the buyer), so the button goes by `withdrawalOpen`, not by the date. Once `withdrawalOpen` is false the server takes no statement of withdrawal; the button then reads something else (for example "Contact us about a return") and leads to the chat of the shop. The page never counts days: it shows the date the server gives and the state it gives.
- **Ask before the statement goes.** The statement of a paid order that was not sent is final at once: the goods go back to the stock, the money back to the card, and the order is cancelled when all of it was named. The page shows a confirmation with the items and units named before it sends the request; the server takes the statement in one step, as the law asks.
- **The money of a return goes through the app, not the dashboard of the payment provider.** `POST /api/returns/:returnId/refund` (or automatically with a cancel of a paid order that was not sent) writes the statement, the stock and the money journal together. A refund made by hand in the dashboard of Stripe is heard by the webhook and written into the money journal, but the app cannot know which goods it was for: an order not sent is held, the transfers of a statement of the order wait, and root says what the refund was ([Payments](payments.md#a-refund-made-by-hand-in-the-dashboard-of-stripe)).

## The statement and its rows

A **statement** (`returns`) is one action: a buyer withdraws from the purchase of some goods of an order, or an admin takes goods back outside the withdrawal. It keeps what the law asks of it: when it was made, by whom and from where (the address and the browser of the request), and the name and the email the answers go to, so the statement lives on after the account is deleted. A statement is never removed. Another statement of the same order can extend it (`extendsId`).

A statement has **rows**, one for each part of an item of the order: the quantity, what became of the goods, what became of the money, the sum. A row starts as the whole of what the statement names and splits when only some of its units get a result (one unit may come back late, another be refused).

| What                                | Values                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Source (`source`)                   | `account` (the buyer, signed in), `form_code` (the public form with the code from a mail), `form_manual` (the public form without the code, checked by an admin by hand), `admin`, `system` (the app cancels an order not sent, see below)                                               |
| Basis (`basis`)                     | `withdrawal` (within the period, the buyer's right), `other` (an admin takes goods back outside it)                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Reason (`reason`)                   | of an admin: `defect`, `wrong_item`, `damaged`, `goodwill`, `shop_cancel`, `refused` (the buyer refused the parcel), `other`; of the system: `dispute_lost` (the bank decided a dispute for the buyer), `stripe_refund` (the whole payment went back by hand in the dashboard of Stripe) |
| Goods of a row                      | `expected`, then `to_stock` (back on the shelf), `written_off`, `lost`, `not_accepted` (with a reason: the shop does not take it back), `not_arrived`                                                                                                                                    |
| Money of a row                      | `not_refunded`, `pending` (on its way), `refunded`, `failed` (the last transfer failed: the reason is in the money journal), `refused`                                                                                                                                                   |
| Status of an order (`returnStatus`) | `none`, `open`, `open_partial`, `partial`, `full`, `declined`                                                                                                                                                                                                                            |
| Close code of rows (`refusedCode`)  | `buyer_changed_mind`, `not_buyer`, `goods_not_returned`, `other` (root), `stated_again` (the database only: the buyer stated the units again, see below)                                                                                                                                 |

A statement closes by itself when every row has its result: the goods are not expected any more and the money is refunded or refused. A row that still waits keeps it open, and the alerts of the admins remind of it; root closes rows that will never get a result without a refund, with a code and a note. The personal data of a statement is cleaned only after it is closed (see below), so a statement left open keeps it.

**The code of a statement.** Each statement has a number in its order (1, 2, 3, set by the database and never changed) and a code made of it, the one the buyer and the admins see: `W` for a withdrawal, `R` for a return outside it, then the order and the number (`W42-2`). Every mail of a statement names it in the subject and the text, the card, the list and its csv file (`code`), the buyer's view of the order and the export carry it, and the search of the list (`q`) finds a statement by its code, in any case.

**A delivered order that came back.** The status of an order does not change with its statements: a delivered order stays `delivered` when all of it came back (only a paid or shipped one becomes `cancelled`). What came back is `returnStatus`, which every place that shows the status of an order gives next to it: the answers of the orders, the list and the card of the admins, the csv of the orders (`Return status`), the profile of a user for the admins, the chat bot.

## The period

- The period starts with the delivery (`deliveredAt`) and lasts the days of the contract the order kept (14 at least; 14 when none was published), counted in calendar days of the time zone of the shop, to the end of the last day. That is the date the buyer is told (`withdrawalEndsAt`, the mails of the order, the bot). The server takes a statement `withdrawalGraceDays` more (root sets it, 4 unless set: an end that falls on a holiday moves, the grace covers it); the bot then says the period of the contract ended and the shop still takes a statement.
- The moment of the delivery is the step to delivered, or the moment the admin gives with it (`deliveredAt` of `PATCH /api/orders/:orderId/status` and of each order of `POST /api/orders/status`: the carrier delivered it before the step was made). It is never of the future and never before the order was sent (`ORDER_DELIVERED_AT_INVALID`), and only goes with the step to delivered (`DELIVERED_AT_NOT_ALLOWED`). It is for the delivery by hand: the admin takes it from the tracking of the carrier, an earlier date shortens the period of the buyer, and the admin log keeps it; the moment from the services of the carriers comes with them.
- Before the delivery (a paid or shipped order) the period has not started and the buyer may always withdraw.
- A paid order that was not sent, and a pickup that is ready and not collected, still have their goods on the shelf: a statement of the buyer (from the account or the form with the code) and a cancel of the shop take the goods back to the stock and the money back at once, and the order is `cancelled` when all of it came back. A statement and the step that sends the order (or hands the pickup over) wait for each other: whichever comes first decides.
- Each item keeps the **rule of withdrawal** it had at the confirmation (`allowed`, `sealed_hygiene`, `sealed_media`, `mixed`, `perishable`, `personalised`, `periodical`); an item without the right cannot be put into a statement, one with a condition (sealed goods) is taken back only if the condition holds.
- The server checks the period when it takes a statement; the days to send the goods (14 from the statement) and to return the money are only watched by the alerts of the admins.

| Root, money admin | `POST /api/payments/money-lines/:moneyLogId/decision`                                                                | what a refund made by hand in the dashboard of Stripe, or what a bank took for a lost dispute, was for: the units of the order it gave back (they pay the rows of the statements first), or a compensation |

There is no route that cancels an order by itself: an order becomes `cancelled` when all of it came back with all the money. The end of a dispute at the bank has no route either: the webhook follows it ([Disputes](disputes.md)). The app makes a statement of its own (`source: system`) only to cancel a paid order that was not sent, when a dispute was lost on it (`dispute_lost`) or its whole payment went back by hand in the dashboard of Stripe (`stripe_refund`): it behaves as a cancel by the shop (the goods back to the stock at once, all that is left of the order, the order `cancelled`), the money that already reached the buyer counts for it, and it has mails of its own ([Payments](payments.md#the-mails)).

The card of a statement tells what it still owes (`moneyLeft`: the rows whose money is owed, the delivery that goes with them and the way back, what of it is due now, what money already with the buyer covers of that — "the bank already gave X for these goods" — what is left of the payment at Stripe, and `short` when a transfer would ask Stripe for more than that), and why the money waits (`disputeOpen`, `handRefundPending`, `bankMoneyPending`). Each line of the list tells what an open statement still has to send (`moneyLeft`, the total of its card; none once it is closed). The list of the statements also finds the ones whose money does not fit in the payment, the way back beside the money the other statements of the order still owe (`moneyShort`) and the ones of an order whose money already with the buyer gave more than its statements took (`moneyOverpaid`). The list of the statements (`GET /api/returns`) filters by the days of the money too, for the money that is due (the goods came back, or the proof of the parcel was confirmed): the 14 days the law gives from the statement are over (`moneyOverdue`) or end within so many days (`moneyDueWithinDays`), and sorts by their end (`sortBy=moneyDeadline`); a statement whose goods are still on their way has no deadline of the money yet. `extended` keeps the statements of a chain of extensions or the ones on their own. The card of a statement for the admins (`GET /api/returns/:returnId`) gives everything the shop knows of it: the rows, the parcels, the account of the order and its recipient, its history from the admin log (root is hidden from an admin there, as in the journals), its money from the money journal (`money`: the event, the purpose, the part, the sum, the state, the time, the id of the refund and the number of the bank; each row names its line, `moneyLogId`), the returned total (`refundedTotal`), and two marks that say why a transfer waits: `disputeOpen` (a dispute of the payment is open at the bank) and `handRefundPending` (a refund made by hand in the dashboard of Stripe waits for root). It also gives two hints: `deductible`, the most the next transfer may still take off for the way back (0 when it may take none), and `warnings` (`shop_paid_parcel`: the shop paid a parcel of the statement back, so a reimbursement of the way back would pay for it twice; it is allowed, the page asks first). The card of an order for the admins (`GET /api/orders/:orderId`) gives an order with statements `returnUnits`: for each item they touched the units bought, refunded, refused, waiting, back with the shop (a unit back with the shop is not to be sent) and left to send (`toShip`), the money that went back and what the order is worth after it. The steps of the status (one order and many) mark an order with statements by its `returnStatus`. The mails "shipped" and "delivered" name only the units that travel.

## Money already with the buyer for goods not taken

Money given back by hand in the dashboard of Stripe or taken by a bank and given to the goods of the order may end up more than the statements of the order take: the shop did not accept goods before root said what the money was for, or lowered a row the money paid in full. The card of each statement of the order tells it (`moneyOverpaid`: what the shares have left for the items whose every unit came back in statements that are over, the delivery with them when every item is so), and the alert `returns_money_overpaid` names the statements to root and the money admins until it is 0. One of them closes it from the card:

- **The goods are still in the shop** (not sent back, no other statement holds their units): `POST /api/returns/:returnId/keep` with `{ items: [{ itemId, goods: to_stock | written_off }], note }`. The goods come in as at the parcel, the row owes its money again and the statement opens again; the money already with the buyer pays the rows it covers all of at once (the buyer is mailed), and what it does not cover goes by the usual transfer, where the value the goods lost may be kept as for any row (`RETURN_KEEP_INVALID`, `RETURN_KEEP_NOT_ALLOWED`).
- **The buyer gave it back outside the app:** `POST /api/returns/:returnId/overpaid/received` with `{ amount, method, note, at }`, at most what is overpaid (`MONEY_RECEIVED_EXCEEDS`). A line `money.received` for each share it pays back, of the purpose of the line of the share and naming it; what the statements had back stays.
- **The buyer will not give it back:** `POST /api/returns/:returnId/overpaid/lost` with `{ note }` (`MONEY_OVERPAID_NONE` when nothing is overpaid). A line `money.share_moved` of 0 for each share (it moves no money): a compensation of a refund made by hand, a loss of what a bank took.

The report by product takes what came back and what was given up off the returns of those goods in the period of the step; what was given up counts among the other refunds of the summary.

## The parcel back

- **One parcel to the shop a statement.** The parcels of the orders and of the statements are one list (`order_shipments`): a parcel of a statement has its `returnId` and goes `to_shop` (the goods come back) or `to_buyer` (the shop sends goods back to the buyer). A statement has at most one parcel `to_shop`: the one the buyer told of (`POST /api/orders/:orderId/returns/:returnId/proof`, the form `POST /api/withdrawals/proof`), the one an admin recorded (`POST /api/returns/:returnId/shipments`), or the one an admin confirms without either. A second one is refused (`409 RETURN_PARCEL_EXISTS`), and so is the buyer telling of a parcel over the one an admin recorded: the buyer corrects only their own. The card of a statement and the buyer's view of the order list them (`parcels`).
- **What the buyer tells.** The carrier and the number, a link to follow the parcel (https), and what they paid the carrier (`buyerPaid`, 0 or more to the cent). That sum is a hint for the shop: the buyer may correct it, with the rest of the parcel, until the shop confirms the parcel.
- **The shop confirms the parcel with who paid the carrier** (`POST /api/returns/:returnId/proof/confirm`, once: `409 RETURN_PROOF_ALREADY_CONFIRMED`). There are two ways. If the shop paid the carrier, `cost` is a line of the money journal on the parcel and nothing is owed to the buyer for it. If the buyer paid, `buyerPaid` is taken from the receipt and replaces what the buyer wrote; it is 0 when there is no receipt. The two sums never come together (`400 RETURN_PARCEL_SUM_INVALID`, as for a sum below 0 or with more than two decimals). When the shop pays the way back (a statement outside the withdrawal, or a withdrawal whose contract does not make the buyer pay it) and did not pay the carrier, the sum of the buyer is required (`400 RETURN_PARCEL_SUM_REQUIRED`): it is what the shop owes the buyer for the way back. When the buyer pays the way back, no sum is needed. The confirmation leaves a line in the admin log (`return.proof_confirm`, the sum given).
- **The goods have a result only with the parcel confirmed.** `to_stock`, `written_off`, `lost` and `not_accepted` need the parcel to the shop confirmed; `not_arrived` needs none. `POST /api/returns/:returnId/receive` confirms a parcel that is not confirmed yet, or writes and confirms one nobody told of, in the same step: `parcel` carries `buyerPaid` or `cost`, with the rules of the confirmation, and the carrier, the number and the link when the admin knows them. Without it the step is refused (`409 RETURN_PARCEL_REQUIRED`). The admin log keeps the carrier of that parcel and never its number.
- **A parcel to the shop only while goods travel.** An admin records the parcel to the shop, or confirms one, only while goods of the statement are on their way to it: a statement whose goods never left the shop (a withdrawal or a cancel before the order was sent: the goods are back on the shelf at once) or are all in has none to write (`409 RETURN_NOTHING_WAITING`).
- **Goods not accepted go back to the buyer in parcels of the statement** (`POST /api/returns/:returnId/shipments` with `direction: to_buyer`). The parcel names the rows of goods not accepted and their units (`items`): a row splits when the parcel carries some of its units, and a unit goes back once (`409 RETURN_NOTHING_TO_SEND_BACK` for a row of other goods, or one sent back already; `RETURN_QUANTITY_EXCEEDED` for more units than the row holds). The statement may be closed already: goods not accepted are its result. The cost the shop paid is a line `money.return_parcel_cost` of the money journal, like the cost of the parcel to the shop, and the delivery report counts it with the parcels back. It is not the way back: a transfer takes none of it off. The card lists the parcel with the rows it carries (`itemIds`); the buyer sees it on the order and is mailed the units, the carrier, the number and the link. The admin log keeps the step (`return.shipment_record`, with the direction and the rows).
- **The goods in more than one parcel: the statement splits.** One parcel goes to the shop a statement, so units sent apart go into a statement of their own. The buyer names the rows and the units the parcel carries (`items` of the proof; the rows are the `id` of `items` of the statement in `withdrawals`): the units that wait and are not named go into a new statement of the order. An admin does the same from the card (`POST /api/returns/:returnId/split`, the units that leave). The new statement keeps the moment, the person, the source, the basis, the reason and the words of the first, with the next number and the first named (`splitFromId`; the first lists it in `splitIds`); its history begins with the split (`return.split` in the admin log). Only rows whose goods wait and whose money did not move leave (`409 RETURN_ITEM_NOT_EXPECTED`, `RETURN_ITEM_MONEY_SETTLED`), a row splits when some of its units leave, and the statement keeps a row (`409 RETURN_SPLIT_INVALID`). The buyer is mailed the code of the new statement and its units, to tell of its parcel. The public form names no units (it shows no rows): an admin splits for such a buyer.
- **Units that did not come in the parcel.** Once the parcel is confirmed, the money of the units that still wait may go ahead of the goods, as with a proof the shop confirmed. When only some of the units came, the admin splits the others off (they come in a parcel of their own), names the rows of the transfer (`itemIds` of the refund), or sets the units that will not come `not_arrived`.

## The money

- **Every movement is a line of the money journal** ([Payments](payments.md#the-money-journal)); the rows of a statement keep only where their money stands (`money`) and the line that put it there (`moneyLogId`), the statement keeps how much went back in all (`refundedTotal`, counted by the database from the journal). A transfer is written in parts that share one key and one refund of Stripe: the goods (below 0, with the rows it pays), the delivery (below 0, with the transfer that makes the order whole), what is kept for the way back (above 0) and what money the buyer already has covers (above 0); the sum of the parts is what Stripe is asked for.
- A transfer has three steps: the database writes its lines in transit under a new key and marks the rows `pending`, Stripe is asked outside the transaction, then its answer settles the lines and the rows (`refunded`, or `failed` with the reason of the failure in a new line of the journal with the opposite sum). A double click is refused; a transfer without an answer stays in transit and is asked again by the worker with the same key, and the webhook of Stripe ends it.
- A transfer Stripe refuses leaves the goods back and the rows `failed` with a code (`refundFailure`): `card_unavailable` (the buyer is mailed that the shop will contact them), `shop_balance`, `disputed` (a dispute at the bank: followed by the app, [Disputes](disputes.md)), `already_refunded` (the money of the payment went back by hand already), `unknown`. The alert of the failed transfers keeps the statement in front of the admins (its list is `GET /api/payments/refunds?purpose=return&state=failed`); an admin tries again, or root records the money as sent outside the app (a way, a note and the real date: a line `money.outside` for each part). A transfer the bank of the card sends back after Stripe took it as done (`refund.failed` of a refund that was sent) is the same `card_unavailable`: the rows are `failed`, the statement opens again, the buyer is told the money did not reach them.
- **Two things hold a transfer back.** While a dispute of the payment is open at the bank, nothing goes through Stripe and nothing is recorded as sent another way (`RETURN_REFUND_DISPUTED`; the card says `disputeOpen`), unless Stripe says the payment can still be refunded: the bank may give the money to the buyer, the shop would pay twice. When the dispute ends the app sends by itself the money that goes by itself (a statement whose goods came back at once: a withdrawal or a cancel of an order not handed over): all of it after a dispute won, what the money of the bank does not cover after one lost; an admin sends the rest as before the dispute ([Disputes](disputes.md)). While a refund made by hand in the dashboard of Stripe waits for root to say what it was for (`RETURN_HAND_REFUND_PENDING`; the card says `handRefundPending`), or what a bank took for a dispute lost on an order sent does (`RETURN_BANK_MONEY_PENDING`), no transfer of the order goes, since it may be this money. Both refusals come for a transfer an admin starts (`/refund`, `/refund/outside`) and only reach the log for the transfers that start by themselves (a withdrawal of the buyer before the order is sent).
- **Money sent outside the app: one step for all a statement owes.** Root or a money admin records it (`POST /api/returns/:returnId/refund/outside`: the way, a note, the real day, the rows or all of them, `returnShippingDeduction` when the buyer owes the way back): the rows whose money is owed, never tried or failed (their goods in, lost, or on their way with the parcel confirmed), the delivery that goes with them, and the way back the shop owes, alone when nothing else is owed. The limit of the payment does not hold here (the debt of the statement does). A transfer sends at most what is left of the payment: when the payment holds less (a compensation or a loss took some of it), Stripe sends what is left, taken off the goods of the last rows (never off what money already with the buyer covers of them), and those rows owe the rest, sent this way; a row is paid in parts (`paid` of `amount` on the card: "paid X of Y") and is refunded once its parts give all of it. With nothing left of the payment the transfer is refused (`409 RETURN_REFUND_EXCEEDS_PAID`) and all of it goes this way, and so does a transfer that takes a deduction for the way back off and does not fit. Money the buyer already has covers its part first, so only the rest is written as sent outside the app. Nothing owed is `409 RETURN_NOTHING_OWED`.
- **Money the buyer already has.** A refund made by hand and the money of a dispute the bank decided for the buyer are of the order; what the decision gave its items and its delivery ([Payments](payments.md#what-money-given-back-by-hand-or-taken-by-a-bank-was-for)) is used first by every transfer of a statement of the order, item by item: Stripe is asked only for the rest, and a transfer they cover in full asks Stripe for nothing (its lines are sent at once, the rows refunded). The decision itself pays at once the rows it covers whole. The returned total counts them, unless that money never reached the buyer.
- **Goods paid ahead that never come.** After the proof of the parcel is confirmed the money may go before the goods arrive. When the parcel then never comes, the goods are `not_arrived` and the money stays `refunded`: the shop gets it back outside the app (from the carrier, or from the buyer). Money still on its way cannot be set so (`RETURN_ITEM_MONEY_SETTLED`).
- The delivery goes back with the last row that makes the order whole (a row whose transfer failed counts, it is still owed: the failed transfer gave the delivery back); once a unit will not come back (not accepted, not arrived), a row that has not sent its money gives its share of the delivery up. The carrier for the parcel back is paid by the buyer or by the shop as the contract the order kept says (`returnShippingPaidBy`; an order that kept no contract told the buyer nothing, so the shop pays). Root lowers a sum with a note where the buyer owes it (the goods lost value; above 0: a row of nothing could never be settled), and each lowered row leaves a line in the admin log (`return.amount_lower`, the sums before and after, the note).
- **The cost of the way back.** When the contract says the buyer pays and the shop paid the carrier (a parcel back with its cost on the statement), an admin or root takes it off a transfer of a withdrawal (`returnShippingDeduction` of the refund): at most what the parcel back to the shop cost less what other transfers took, and the transfer stays above 0. When the shop pays and the buyer paid the carrier, the shop owes the buyer the sum of the receipt the admin set on the parcel (`buyerPaid`, see [The parcel back](#the-parcel-back)): it goes with the transfer that ends the money of the statement as a part of its own (`way_back`, below 0), when the payment holds it with the rest and with the money the other statements of the order still owe; the goods go first, so a way back that does not fit stays owed. Left alone (no row of the statement owed), it goes by a transfer of its own (a refund without `itemIds`) once the payment holds it; one that can never fit (a statement for all of the order, say) is sent outside the app. A failed transfer gives it back, the next transfer takes it again. The cost of a parcel back is a line `money.return_parcel_cost` (written with the parcel or later; a change is a line `money.correction` of the difference; a cost of 0 writes none). The buyer is told of both in the mail of the money (the way back alone, sent by a transfer of its own or outside the app, has a mail of its own); the reports count the deduction as an income of the delivery and the way back paid as spent, by the time their lines were written.
- **The free delivery is not counted again.** A buyer who withdraws from part of a paid order before it is sent keeps the free delivery the whole order earned, even when the rest is below the threshold: the shop pays that delivery, the transfer takes nothing for it.
- **Goods that came back damaged or used.** The admin sets the units `not_accepted` with a reason (`unsealed`, `mixed`, `used`, `damaged`, `other`): their money is refused and the stock does not move. The shop sends them back to the buyer in a parcel of the statement (see [The parcel back](#the-parcel-back)), or keeps them, as the shop and the buyer agree. A loss of value the buyer owes (the goods were used more than needed to check them) is not a refusal: root lowers the sum of the row with a note, and the buyer is told the sum kept, never the note.
- The reports count an event in the period it happened in, and a past period never changes (see [features](features.md)).

## The public form and the code

The buyer of a deleted account, or one who did not sign in, withdraws by the public form of the frontend (`WEB_PAGE_WITHDRAWAL`). The mails of the order carry a link with a code after `#`: the code is the HMAC of the number of the order (`WITHDRAWAL_CODE_SECRET`), stored nowhere. A statement with the code is taken like one from the account; one without it (or with a wrong one) is kept for an admin to check by hand, and a paid order is not cancelled by it. Every answer to a wrong request is the same, and the form is limited by address, and by order from every address for the requests without the code of the order (a request with the code is the buyer's: nobody can use up their requests).

**The units of the form.** The page of the form shows the units the visitor may withdraw from (`POST /api/withdrawals/items`) once they showed the order is theirs, and the form checks the same facts again when it is sent. The number of the order is always needed. With the code: the name of the recipient (also for an account that is alive, without a login). Without the code: the name, the phone of the recipient and, for a delivery to an address, its country and its postal code when the order has one; a pickup has the name and the phone only. The answer has only the units with the right that no statement holds, while the period is open, without prices; every failure is the same answer and the server does the same work whatever was wrong. Besides the limits by address and by order, the requests without the code count by the facts of the recipient (`LIMIT_WITHDRAWAL_FORM_PER_DATA`: the phone, or the name), from every address. A person without the number of the order withdraws by a letter or in the chat; the contract or the privacy policy should say the form needs the number and the other ways stay open.

**The check of an admin.** A statement without the code waits for an admin, who checks it outside the app and accepts it (`POST /api/returns/:returnId/accept`, once; the card shows `acceptedAt` and `acceptedBy`) or root closes it as `not_buyer`. Until it is accepted, its goods, its proof, its money, a parcel and the way back are refused (`409 RETURN_NOT_ACCEPTED`), and it is in the alert `returns_manual_check` (the list filter `accepted=false`). One statement without the code waits per order (the database holds it): another gets `409 WITHDRAWAL_UNDER_REVIEW`, told only once the facts matched; once the first is accepted, another may come for the units left.

**A statement without the code gives way to the buyer.** Anybody who knows the number of an order and the name of its recipient can send the form without the code, so its units are held only until the buyer states them: a statement from the account or with the code takes the units it needs from an open statement without the code (oldest rows first). Only a statement no admin accepted yet gives its units up: an accepted one is the buyer's. Those units leave it: `not_arrived`, the money refused, the code `stated_again`. The rest of it stays open for the admin, and a statement left with nothing is closed by its results. The person of the statement without the code is mailed that its goods are now handled in another statement of the order, without who made it and without the units; the line of the new statement in the admin log names the statements it took them from (`takenFrom`). The buyer's view of the order counts those units as free to withdraw from. A statement of an admin takes nothing over.

## Mails

The buyer is mailed when a statement is made (with the date, the address to send the goods to, the last day to send them, who pays the carrier), when the shop made one, when the money went back (a transfer to the card adds that the shop will write if anything happens to it: a refund the bank of the card sends back later brings the mail of the failed transfer; money root sent outside the app promises nothing), when the transfer failed (`card_unavailable`), when root paid back the way back, and when the statement is closed (the money told by where it came from: what the bank gave back for a dispute the shop lost, what went to the way the buyer paid, with "it goes back the way you paid", and each sum root sent outside the app with its way and its day; when the money of a transfer closes the statement, this mail is the only one); the person of a statement without the code is mailed when the buyer took its units over. A statement of the system has its own mail in place of these, once its rows are refunded: "the order is cancelled and the money sent" after the whole payment went back by hand, "the order is cancelled after the decision of your bank" (what the bank gave back, what the shop sent of the rest) after a dispute lost ([Payments](payments.md#the-mails)). Each mail names the code of the statement, and gives its dates in the time zone of the shop, which it names (`SHOP_TIME_ZONE`). When root lowered a sum, the mail of the money and the mail of the end say how much was kept for the value the goods lost and for which goods, never the note of root. The mails of the order (paid, shipped, delivered) carry the days of the contract or the date until which the buyer may withdraw (the end of the contract, without the grace) and the link of the form. A mail about a statement goes to the email of the statement, also after the account was deleted; a mail of a statement of the form is greeted by the name of the form, and one of a statement without the code names no goods. Every mail of the shop ends with the same line: write in the chat of the site or to `emailReplyTo` (root sets it).

## What stays where, and for how long

A statement and its rows are never removed: the reports, the stock log and the batches stand on them. What is **personal** is emptied after the term the privacy policy gives. Root publishes the text of the policy together with the terms in days (`POST /api/root/legal-documents`, kind `privacy`), a version is never changed, and the page shows the numbers from the fields of the current one. A piece of data is kept for the **shorter** of the term of the version in force when it was collected and of the version in force now, and is cleaned a day before the term ends. Without a published policy nothing is cleaned (but for the requests to subscribe that nobody confirmed, which have no term in the policy). The cleanups are functions of the database that a periodic task is to run once a day as the system; the service of periodic tasks is not built yet, so nothing runs them today.

**What the text of the policy says about the newsletters.** The terms above are fields of the version; the text is root's, and for the newsletters it should say four things: the admins who run the newsletters (root and the admins root allowed) see the emails of the subscribers; the list of the addresses the subscription form refuses (a person said the address is not theirs, or an admin added it) keeps only a keyed hash of each address, with no term, until an admin removes the record; the proof of the consent (the subscription with the journal of its events: when, the address and the browser, the version of the policy in force) is kept for `retentionNewsletterConsentDays` days after the subscription ended; and the rows of the mails of a campaign are kept for `retentionNewsletterRecipientsDays` days after the campaign ended. The export of a person (`GET /api/users/me/export`) gives the subscriptions of their address with their journals for as long as they are kept, and nothing of what a cleanup removed.

An order that is paid or shipped, and an order with an open statement, keeps its recipient until it is settled; so does a delivered order while its period of withdrawal may run (the days of its contract, the longest grace of 30 days and a day for the time zone), because the public form asks for the name of the recipient. An open statement keeps its data while it is open. The journals keep no words of the buyer and no number or link of a parcel (those stay on the statement only); the address and the browser of the requests of the buyer or of the form that the journal of the admins, the stock log and the money journal keep for a statement are emptied with those of the statement, the lines of the admins keep theirs. The account is deleted at once (`DELETE /api/users/me`), also with an open order or an open statement, and the right of withdrawal stays: the statement goes on without the account, with mails to its email and the money to the card of the order.

**Access and portability.** A signed-in person downloads their data with `GET /api/users/me/export`, which gives every order its statements: their code, their words, their data, the parcel they sent, the rows with the money; and the money of the orders of the person from the money journal (`money`: the order, the event, the part, the sum, the currency, the time, the state, the number of the bank; not what the shop paid the carriers). It does not give what became of the goods (only why the money of a row was refused), what the shop paid to carry them back, the ids of the provider, the notes of the admins, and the address and the browser of a statement an admin made (those are the admin's). Of a statement of the form without the code it gives what its sender wrote (the contact, the address, the browser, the words) only when the shop took it for the buyer's (money went back for it) or its email is the one of the account, never when root closed it as not of the buyer. For a deleted account, or a request by mail, root uses `GET /api/users/:userId/export` (the same set; the person is checked outside the app: the email of the statement, the phone of the recipient, the number of an order) and the request leaves a line in the admin log. What the cleanups have emptied is not given.

## The chat bot

The bot knows each order of a signed-in customer with its statements: whether the customer may still withdraw, until when (the end of the contract; in the days of grace it says the period ended and the shop still takes a statement), for which units, and where the money is. It never files or promises a return, and points to the button of withdrawal. An order whose transfer of money failed, an order something holds from its sending and an order with an open dispute of its payment are left to an admin. When the bot cannot answer a user with a confirmed email (the model gave no answer, or a limit of the shop or of the customer is used up), `GET /api/chat/thread/current` has `adminCallAvailable: true` and the socket sends `thread:admin-call`: the page shows the button "call an admin" (`POST /api/chat/threads/:threadId/call-admin`). The button comes back every time the bot is down and goes away when it answers again.
